> **TL;DR** — Berberine activates AMPK by mildly inhibiting mitochondrial Complex I; resveratrol allosterically activates SIRT1. The two enzymes aren't independent — AMPK activation raises the NAD+/NADH ratio that SIRT1 needs, and SIRT1 deacetylates LKB1, the upstream kinase that keeps AMPK switched on. Each compound's own module already names this pairing; no dedicated combination RCT exists. AM berberine with meals, PM resveratrol with fat. Tier B — mechanistic crosstalk is textbook, the specific combination is not trial-tested.

## 1. Why this stack exists

**AMPK** and **SIRT1** are the cell's two primary energy-stress sensors, and they aren't independent switches — they run a bidirectional feedback loop. AMPK activation increases the NAD+/NADH ratio (partly via NAMPT-driven NAD+ salvage), which is the exact cofactor SIRT1 requires to deacetylate its targets. SIRT1, in turn, deacetylates and activates LKB1 — the upstream kinase that phosphorylates and activates AMPK in the first place. Each enzyme reinforces the other's activation state.

| Layer | Compound | Role | Analogy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Energy-stress sensor** | Berberine | Mild Complex I inhibition → AMPK activation | Alarm |
| **Deacetylase activator** | Resveratrol | Allosteric SIRT1 boost | Amplifier |

**Gap analysis — what breaks when you use only one:**

- Berberine alone → AMPK activates, glucose/lipid metabolism improves, but SIRT1-mediated biogenesis and epigenetic programs get only the indirect NAD+/NADH boost, not direct activation
- Resveratrol alone → SIRT1 stimulated, but without an independent AMPK trigger the LKB1→AMPK arm of the loop is weaker
- Combined → both entry points into the same feedback loop are engaged, rather than relying on one enzyme to indirectly recruit the other

## 2. Mechanistic synergy map

```
AM (with meals): [Berberine] → mild Complex I inhibition → ↑AMP:ATP ratio → AMPK ON
                       ↓
                 AMPK ON → ↑NAD+/NADH ratio (NAMPT salvage) → fuels SIRT1
                       ↓
PM (with fat):   [Resveratrol] → allosteric SIRT1 activation
                       ↓
Output: SIRT1 deacetylates LKB1 → LKB1 re-activates AMPK (positive feedback)
        PGC-1α deacetylation → mitochondrial biogenesis
        NF-κB deacetylation → ↓ inflammaging
```

**Hallmarks addressed:** Mitochondrial dysfunction · Deregulated nutrient sensing · Chronic inflammation · Epigenetic alterations — berberine's own module and resveratrol's own module each name the other as a complementary pairing; this page is the mechanism deep-dive neither one has room for.

## 3. Evidence per leg

| Compound | Tier | Key human data |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Berberine | A | Head-to-head RCT vs metformin, 116 T2D patients (PMID 18396172); meta-analysis of 27 RCTs (PMID 26507383) |
| Resveratrol | B | Strong SIRT1/AMPK mechanism (PMID 30930169); mixed standalone human outcomes |
| Combination | — | No completed RCT; AMPK↔SIRT1 crosstalk is established cell/animal biology, not a tested human combination |

**Combination logic:** Berberine's glucose/lipid effects (Tier A) and resveratrol's SIRT1 mechanism (Tier B) are each independently well-evidenced. The *combination* is graded on mechanism plausibility alone — same standard as the site's other mechanistic-pair stacks — until a dedicated trial exists.

:::evidence tier="B"
Yin et al. 2008 (PMID 18396172): berberine 500 mg TID matched metformin for glycemic control in a 13-week RCT. Animal mechanism studies (PMID 17298901) establish berberine's Complex I → AMPK mechanism. PMID 30930169 covers the AMPK/SIRT1/NAD+ crosstalk this pairing depends on. No combination RCT exists — pair graded Tier B on mechanism strength alone.
:::

## 4. AM/PM choreography (non-negotiable spacing)

| Step | Time | Compound | Dose | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | With breakfast + dinner | Berberine | 500 mg BID → TID | Split dosing reduces GI effects; titrate over 2 weeks |
| 2 | PM dinner (with fat) | Trans-resveratrol | 150 mg → 250–500 mg | ≥15 g dietary fat for absorption |
| 3 | Optional week 8+ | NMN AM fasted | 250 mg | Feeds the same NAD+/NADH pool AMPK is raising — see [SIRT1 pair](/library/synergies/nmn-resveratrol-sirt1) |

**Why spacing matters:** Berberine's glucose-lowering effect peaks around meals, where it does double duty blunting postprandial glucose excursions. Resveratrol's fat-dependent absorption makes it a natural PM pairing with dinner, matching the same choreography as the SIRT1 pair.

:::practical title="Day-one setup (15 minutes)"
1. Split berberine into two meal-anchored doses, not one large dose (GI tolerance)
2. Label PM resveratrol bottle for the dinner meal specifically
3. Baseline fasting glucose and HbA1c before starting
4. If already on the [SIRT1 pair](/library/synergies/nmn-resveratrol-sirt1), berberine layers onto the existing PM resveratrol dose — no schedule change needed
5. Read [Berberine](/library/compounds/berberine) and [Resveratrol](/library/compounds/resveratrol) deep dives
:::

## 6. Red flags and contraindications

:::redflag title="Hard stops — do not run this stack without clearance"
- **Insulin, sulfonylureas, or any glucose-lowering medication** — combined AMPK activation risks hypoglycemia
- **Any CYP3A4-metabolized prescription drug** — berberine is a potent CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitor and can raise drug levels unpredictably
- **Pregnancy or breastfeeding** — berberine may cross the placenta; insufficient safety data for the combination
- **Scheduled surgery within 2 weeks** — resveratrol's theoretical platelet effects
:::

:::warning title="Soft cautions"
Berberine GI effects (cramping, loose stool) are common — split into BID/TID dosing with meals rather than one large dose. Resveratrol GI upset — take with the largest fat-containing meal. Start berberine alone for 1–2 weeks before layering resveratrol, so any GI response is attributable to one compound.
:::
