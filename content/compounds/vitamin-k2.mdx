> **TL;DR** — Vitamin K2 (specifically the MK-7 form) activates matrix Gla protein (MGP), which directs calcium toward bone and away from arterial walls. The Rotterdam Study found higher dietary K2 intake associated with less coronary calcification and lower cardiovascular mortality; the mechanistic case for pairing K2 with vitamin D3/calcium is strong. Human RCT outcome data (hard cardiovascular events) is thinner than the observational case. Tier B; a rational pairing with vitamin D3, not a standalone proven intervention.

## 1. What vitamin K2 does

Vitamin K has two relevant forms: K1 (phylloquinone, from leafy greens, mainly used for blood clotting) and **K2 (menaquinones, especially the long-chain MK-7 form)**, found in fermented foods (natto) and animal products, with a longer half-life and broader tissue distribution than K1. K2's distinguishing role is activating **matrix Gla protein (MGP)**, a protein that inhibits calcium deposition in arterial walls and soft tissue — meaning K2 status plausibly determines whether circulating calcium goes toward bone (where you want it) or arteries (where you don't).

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** [Loss of proteostasis](/library/loss-of-proteostasis) (bone matrix) · [Chronic inflammation](/library/chronic-inflammation) (vascular calcification)

:::evidence tier="B"
The Rotterdam Study (large Dutch observational cohort) found higher dietary K2 (not K1) intake associated with less coronary artery calcification and lower cardiovascular mortality. Smaller human RCTs (e.g., Knapen et al.) show K2 supplementation slows the progression of arterial stiffness markers over 2–3 years. This is a genuinely compelling mechanistic and observational case, but large RCTs with hard cardiovascular-event endpoints for K2 supplementation specifically are still limited. Tier B: strong mechanism plus supportive human data, short of definitive outcome-trial proof.
:::

## 2. Mechanism

| Pathway | Mechanism | Hallmark link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MGP activation | Gamma-carboxylates matrix Gla protein, enabling it to inhibit arterial calcification | Loss of proteostasis |
| Osteocalcin activation | Also activates osteocalcin, directing calcium into bone matrix | Loss of proteostasis |
| Vascular calcium regulation | Observational link to reduced coronary artery calcification | Chronic inflammation |

:::practical title="The natural pairing with vitamin D3"
Vitamin D3 increases calcium absorption; K2 helps direct where that calcium goes. If you supplement vitamin D3 at meaningful doses, pairing it with K2 (MK-7) is a mechanistically sound, commonly recommended combination — many combined D3+K2 products exist for exactly this reason.
:::

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study | Design | Key outcomes | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rotterdam Study | Large observational cohort | Higher dietary K2 (not K1) associated with less coronary calcification, lower CV mortality | B (observational) |
| Knapen et al. (arterial stiffness RCTs) | Human RCT, 2–3 years | Slowed progression of arterial stiffness markers with K2 supplementation | B |

**Consensus:** Tier B. Strong mechanism and supportive observational/intermediate-outcome data; hard-endpoint RCT confirmation is still developing.

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Dose:** 90–180 mcg/day of MK-7 (the long-half-life form used in most positive human data).
- **Pairing:** commonly combined with vitamin D3; take together with a fat-containing meal (fat-soluble vitamin).
- **Duration:** vascular-marker effects were measured over years in the supportive RCTs — this is a long-horizon compound.

:::decision title="Should you add vitamin K2?"
node | Are you supplementing vitamin D3 or calcium at meaningful doses?
yes | Pair with K2 (MK-7) 90–180 mcg/day — the mechanistically sound combination
no | Lower priority alone, though dietary K2 (natto, some cheeses) is reasonable regardless
redflag | On warfarin or another vitamin-K-antagonist anticoagulant → vitamin K2 directly interferes with these drugs; do not supplement without physician coordination
:::

## 5. Monitoring

There's no simple consumer blood test for vitamin K2 status specifically — track the outcomes it's meant to support.

| Measure | Target | Frequency | Action if off-target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Coronary artery calcium score (if obtained) | Lower / stable | Per physician schedule | Progressing → discuss with physician; K2 is supportive, not a substitute for risk-factor management |
| Bone density (DEXA, if relevant) | Stable / improving | Per physician schedule | Declining → broader bone-health workup, not K2 alone |

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Well tolerated** at supplemental doses; no established upper-limit toxicity at typical MK-7 doses.
- **Warfarin and other vitamin-K antagonists** — vitamin K2 directly opposes these drugs' mechanism; this is an absolute coordination point, not optional.
- **Blood-clotting disorders** — coordinate with a physician before supplementing.

:::redflag title="Do not combine with vitamin-K-antagonist anticoagulants without physician guidance"
- **On warfarin** — vitamin K2 directly interferes with warfarin's mechanism; dose changes require physician-supervised INR monitoring
- **Clotting disorders** — physician oversight before supplementing
:::
