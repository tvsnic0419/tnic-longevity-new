> **TL;DR** — Urolithin A is a gut-derived metabolite that activates mitophagy, the selective recycling of damaged mitochondria. It is the only mitophagy-specific compound with human RCT data. Tier A for muscle-function endpoints. 500–1,000 mg/day with food; retest muscle endurance and hs-CRP at 12–16 weeks.

## 1. What urolithin A does (and why it matters)

Urolithin A is produced when gut bacteria metabolize ellagitannins from pomegranate, walnuts, and berries — but only about 40% of people host the microbiome needed to make meaningful amounts. Direct supplementation bypasses that lottery. Its defining action is mitophagy induction: the quality-control process that clears dysfunctional mitochondria so healthy ones can replace them. Because mitophagy declines with age, urolithin A targets one of the more mechanistically clean nodes in mitochondrial aging.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** Mitochondrial dysfunction · Disabled macroautophagy · Cellular senescence

:::evidence tier="A"
PMID 35391504 — Randomized trial of urolithin A in healthy older adults (*Cell Reports Medicine*, 2022). 1,000 mg/day improved muscle endurance and lowered plasma acylcarnitines and inflammatory markers. First-in-human safety and mitochondrial data: PMID 31230029 (*Cell Metabolism*, 2019).
:::

## 2. Mechanism — targeted mitochondrial recycling

Urolithin A upregulates mitophagy genes, tagging depolarized mitochondria for autophagic clearance. The net effect is a younger, more efficient mitochondrial pool — measurable in humans as improved muscle endurance and reduced circulating markers of mitochondrial stress.

| Pathway | Mechanism | Hallmark link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mitophagy induction | Clears depolarized mitochondria for replacement | Mitochondrial dysfunction |
| Autophagy support | Complements broad autophagic flux | Disabled macroautophagy |
| Inflammaging | Lowers hs-CRP and acylcarnitines in RCTs | Cellular senescence |

:::practical title="When urolithin A is worth your money"
Urolithin A earns a stack slot when **two or more** apply:

- Age 40+ with declining exercise endurance or recovery
- Low dietary ellagitannin intake or a non-converting microbiome
- You want a mitophagy-specific agent alongside NAD+ restoration

**Skip or defer** if your budget is limited — this is a premium-priced compound; a Tier A foundation (NMN, GlyNAC) comes first.

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study | Design | Key outcomes | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Liu 2022 (PMID 35391504) | RCT, older adults, 4 mo | Muscle endurance ↑; acylcarnitines and CRP ↓ | A |
| Andreux 2019 (PMID 31230029) | First-in-human RCT | Safe; dose-dependent mitochondrial gene expression | A |
| Ryu 2016 (PMID 27400265) | Preclinical | Mitophagy induction; extended lifespan in *C. elegans* | B |

**Consensus:** Tier A for muscle-function and mitochondrial-biomarker endpoints — the human trials are consistent and biopsy-confirmed. Longevity itself remains extrapolated from the mitophagy mechanism and animal lifespan data.

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Start:** 500 mg/day with a fat-containing meal
- **Target:** 500–1,000 mg/day (the 1,000 mg dose matches the muscle-endurance RCT)
- **Form:** Standardized urolithin A (e.g., Mitopure) — not pomegranate extract, which most people cannot convert
- **Timing:** With food; consistent daily dosing (not pulsed)
- **Duration:** 12–16 weeks before judging endurance changes

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Well tolerated:** Human RCTs report no significant adverse events at 500–1,000 mg/day over four months.
- **Premium cost:** The main practical constraint — standardized urolithin A is expensive relative to its Tier B longevity certainty.
- **Sourcing:** Pomegranate extract is not equivalent; most people cannot convert it to urolithin A.

:::redflag title="Do not self-start without clearance"
- **Pregnancy or breastfeeding** — insufficient supplemental-dose safety data
- **Active cancer treatment** — mitophagy modulation should be physician-coordinated
- **Rely on "pomegranate extract" claims** — verify standardized urolithin A content on the COA
:::
