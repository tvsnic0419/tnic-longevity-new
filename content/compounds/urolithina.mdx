> **TL;DR** — Urolithin A is a gut-derived ellagitannin metabolite that activates mitophagy — selective clearance of damaged mitochondria — independent of AMPK or mTOR. Only 30–40% of adults produce meaningful amounts from diet alone, which is the core rationale for direct supplementation. First human evidence came from a 2019 safety/biomarker trial; a 2022 randomized trial added functional outcomes. 500–1000 mg AM (Mitopure standardized) with a fat-containing meal.

## 1. What urolithin A does (and why it matters)

Pomegranates and walnuts contain ellagitannins, which gut bacteria convert into urolithin A — but that conversion depends on having the right microbiota, and only an estimated 30–40% of adults are efficient "producers." For the other 60–70%, dietary ellagitannin intake alone does not reliably raise urolithin A levels. Direct supplementation with a standardized form bypasses this bottleneck entirely.

Once absorbed, urolithin A activates mitophagy: the selective autophagic clearance of damaged mitochondria, distinct from bulk (macro)autophagy and operating through a separate signaling route from AMPK or mTOR.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** Mitochondrial dysfunction · Disabled autophagy (mitophagy specifically) · Cellular senescence

:::evidence tier="A"
Andreux PA et al., *Nature Metabolism* 2019 (PMID 32694802): first human clinical evidence that an oral compound is safe and induces a molecular signature of improved mitochondrial and cellular health in skeletal muscle of middle-aged/older adults. This is a safety and biomarker trial, not a functional-outcomes trial.
:::

## 2. Mechanism — gut metabolite to mitophagy activator

### 2a. From ellagitannins to circulating urolithin A

| Step | What happens | Hallmark link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dietary ellagitannins | Pomegranate, walnuts, some berries | Substrate only — not active itself |
| Gut microbial conversion | Only ~30–40% of adults convert efficiently | Explains supplementation rationale |
| Direct urolithin A supplementation | Bypasses variable microbiome step | Reliable dosing regardless of gut flora |

### 2b. Mitophagy activation

:::pathway title="Urolithin A mitophagy pathway" nodes="Urolithin A|Gut/supplement-derived|cyan,PINK1/Parkin axis|Damage sensor|amber,Mitophagy|Selective clearance|violet,Mitochondrial quality|Outcome|emerald" edges="Urolithin A->PINK1/Parkin axis|suppresses brakes on,PINK1/Parkin axis->Mitophagy|enables,Mitophagy->Mitochondrial quality|improves"
:::

Urolithin A suppresses the brakes on the PINK1/Parkin axis, the cell's primary damaged-mitochondria tagging system, allowing mitophagy to proceed. This is mechanistically distinct from AMPK-mediated general autophagy (as with berberine or resveratrol) — urolithin A's action is specific to mitochondrial quality control rather than broad nutrient-sensing signaling.

Ryu D et al., *Nature Metabolism* 2016 (PMID 27400265) — a preclinical study in *C. elegans* and rodents — first demonstrated that urolithin A induces mitophagy, prolongs lifespan in worms, and increases muscle function in rodents. This is animal data only and should not be read as a human lifespan or muscle-function finding.

:::practical title="When urolithin A earns a stack slot"
Urolithin A has clear value when **two or more** apply:

- Mitochondrial dysfunction is a top hallmark, especially with age-related muscle/exercise capacity decline
- You eat little to no pomegranate/walnut, or don't know your gut-conversion status
- Running a mitochondrial-focused stack alongside NMN or spermidine
- You can commit to consistent AM dosing with a fat-containing meal

**Skip or defer:** if mitochondrial dysfunction isn't a priority hallmark for you; budget-constrained stacks may prioritize NMN first and add urolithin A once foundational NAD+ support is established.
:::

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study | Design | Population | Key outcomes | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Andreux 2019 (PMID 32694802) | Human safety/biomarker trial | Middle-aged/older adults | Safe; induced molecular signature of improved mitochondrial/cellular health in skeletal muscle | A |
| Singh 2022 (PMID 35584623) | Human RCT, functional outcomes | Middle-aged adults | Improved muscle strength, exercise performance, mitochondrial health biomarkers vs placebo | A |
| Ryu 2016 (PMID 27400265) | Preclinical | *C. elegans*, rodents | Induced mitophagy, extended lifespan in worms, increased muscle function in rodents | — |

**Consensus:** Tier A — this is one of the stronger human-evidence compounds on the panel. Andreux 2019 established human safety and a mitochondrial biomarker signature; Singh 2022 independently confirmed functional outcomes (strength, exercise performance) in a randomized trial. The Ryu 2016 lifespan-extension finding is preclinical (worm/rodent) and has not been replicated as a human longevity endpoint — do not extrapolate the worm lifespan result to humans.

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Excellent safety profile** across Phase 1/2 human trials at studied doses (500–1000 mg)
- **Take with a fat-containing meal** — absorption depends on it
- **No known major drug interactions** reported in trials to date

:::redflag title="Consult before starting"
- **Pregnant or nursing** — insufficient safety data specific to pregnancy/nursing populations
- **Active cancer treatment** — coordinate with your oncologist before adding any mitophagy-modulating compound
- **Taking immunomodulating therapy** — consult your prescriber given the compound's effects on cellular clearance pathways
:::

:::warning title="Soft cautions"
No significant adverse events have been reported in the cited human trials at 500–1000 mg. The main practical failure mode is skipping the fat-containing meal, which reduces absorption.
:::
