> **TL;DR** — Citrus bergamot is a polyphenol-rich extract with real human RCTs showing meaningful drops in LDL and triglycerides and a rise in HDL — a genuinely useful, non-statin lever for lipids and metabolic health. It works through the same HMG-CoA pathway statins target, plus AMPK activation. 500–1,000 mg/day standardized to flavonoids.

## 1. What citrus bergamot does (and why it matters)

Bergamot (*Citrus bergamia*) concentrates a distinctive set of flavonoids — brutieridin and melitidin — that behave like natural HMG-CoA reductase modulators. In human trials it lowers total and LDL cholesterol, drops triglycerides, and nudges HDL upward, often by double-digit percentages. For anyone building a metabolic or cardiovascular layer without (or alongside) a statin, that is a strong, well-tolerated option.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** Deregulated nutrient sensing · Chronic inflammation · Altered intercellular communication

## 2. Mechanism

The bergamot flavonoids inhibit HMG-CoA reductase (the rate-limiting step in cholesterol synthesis) and activate AMPK, improving lipid handling and insulin sensitivity. They also act as antioxidants that reduce LDL oxidation — the form of LDL that actually drives plaque.

## 3. Evidence

Multiple placebo-controlled RCTs (Mollace and colleagues, and later independent trials) report LDL and triglyceride reductions in the 15–30% range at 500–1,000 mg/day over 4–12 weeks, with additive effects when stacked on a statin. Effect size varies by extract standardization, which is the main quality lever.

## 4. Dosing

500–1,000 mg/day of an extract standardized to ~38% flavonoids, taken before the largest meal. Give it 8–12 weeks before judging.

## 5. Monitoring

Recheck a fasting lipid panel (LDL, triglycerides, HDL, and ideally ApoB) at baseline and 12 weeks. Fasting glucose/HbA1c if you are using it for metabolic support.

## 6. Safety & red flags

Well tolerated; mild GI upset is the most common complaint. Unlike grapefruit, standardized bergamot extract is low in the furanocoumarins that cause CYP3A4 drug interactions — but if you take narrow-therapeutic-index medications, confirm your product is furanocoumarin-reduced and clear it with a clinician.

## 7. Synergies & stack integration

Complements **berberine** (AMPK/glucose) and **omega-3** (triglycerides) for a metabolic stack, and stacks additively on statins for LDL. Pair with a fiber/prebiotic base for bile-acid–driven cholesterol clearance.
