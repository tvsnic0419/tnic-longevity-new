> **TL;DR** — Phosphatidylserine is a membrane phospholipid essential to neuronal signaling. Human trials show cognitive support in aging (Crook 1991, PMID 1876787) and cortisol dampening after exercise stress (Starks 2008, PMID 18662395). Modern soy-derived PS at 100–300 mg/day is safe and well-tolerated; the older bovine-cortex PS with the classic dementia trials is no longer available.

## 1. What phosphatidylserine does (and why it matters)

Phosphatidylserine (PS) is a **phospholipid asymmetrically enriched on the inner leaflet** of every cell membrane, most concentrated in the brain (~15% of total brain phospholipid mass). PS anchors neuronal signaling scaffolds (PKC, Raf-1), regulates the "eat-me" signal that flags apoptotic cells for phagocytosis, and stabilizes the docking of synaptic vesicles during neurotransmitter release.

Brain PS declines with age, and the classical rationale for supplementation was to replete this pool. Modern soy- and sunflower-derived PS is what's on the market now (the original bovine-cortex PS behind the 1990s dementia trials was withdrawn after BSE concerns) — the fatty-acid composition differs, but the polar head group (serine) is what matters biologically.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** Altered intercellular communication (synaptic signaling) · Loss of proteostasis (membrane integrity) · Chronic inflammation (cortisol/HPA axis)

:::evidence tier="B"
Crook et al. (1991, PMID 1876787) randomized 149 adults with age-associated memory impairment to bovine PS 300 mg/day vs placebo for 12 weeks — the treatment arm improved on name/face matching and learning tasks (largest gains in the most-impaired quartile). Starks et al. (2008, PMID 18662395) showed 600 mg PS/day for 10 days blunted the post-exercise cortisol spike by ~30% in trained males.
:::

## 2. Mechanism — membrane phospholipid vs stress hormone

| Pathway | Mechanism | Hallmark link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Membrane composition | Restores PS in aging neuronal membranes | Proteostasis |
| Synaptic signaling | Anchors PKC, activates Raf-1 → MAPK cascade | Communication |
| ACTH/cortisol | Dampens ACTH release; blunts cortisol response to physical stress | Communication |
| Apoptotic flag | PS externalization marks cells for phagocytosis (this is regulated, not disrupted by supplementation) | — |

The cortisol effect is the most reproducible acute finding: multiple crossover studies in trained males show 400–800 mg PS/day dampens the cortisol response to intense exercise without blunting the training adaptation itself.

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study | Design | N | Duration | Key outcomes | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Crook 1991 (PMID 1876787) | RCT (bovine PS) | 149 | 12 wk | ↑ memory, learning in age-associated impairment | B |
| Starks 2008 (PMID 18662395) | Crossover | 10 | 10 d | ↓ Post-exercise cortisol; ↑ testosterone:cortisol ratio | B |
| Hellhammer 2004 (PMID 15512856) | RCT | 60 | 42 d | ↓ ACTH, ↓ cortisol response to mental stress | B |
| Vakhapova 2010 (PMID 20130432) | RCT (soy PS + DHA) | 157 | 15 wk | Modest verbal recall gains in age-related complaint | C |

**Consensus:** Tier B for the cortisol-dampening effect and for cognitive support in genuinely-impaired older adults. Modern soy PS carries thinner evidence than the withdrawn bovine PS — assume smaller effect sizes.

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Excellent safety profile;** GI upset (~5%) at high doses, resolves with food.
- **Modern PS is soy or sunflower-derived** — safe in soy-sensitive individuals if sunflower source verified.

:::redflag title="Do not self-start without clearance"
- **On anticoagulants** — theoretical additive effect at very high doses (>800 mg/day).
- **Addison's disease or on hydrocortisone replacement** — cortisol-dampening property is contraindicated.
:::
