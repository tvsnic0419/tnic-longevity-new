## 1. What Low-Dose Lithium does

Low-Dose Lithium is best understood through **gsk-3 and autophagy signaling**. Ecological and preclinical signals are hypothesis-generating only; even low exposure can affect thyroid and kidney function, and lithium orotate lacks robust longevity trials. The evidence base below is anchored to PubMed records returned for this intervention (PMID 41770546, PMID 22746245, PMID 38253184); none proves that the compound extends human lifespan.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** autophagy · communication

:::evidence tier="C"
The rating reflects the gap between pathway or condition-specific outcomes and direct longevity evidence. A positive surrogate, symptom, or disease trial should not be promoted into a lifespan claim.
:::

## 2. Mechanism and biological context

| Layer | What is supported | What remains uncertain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exposure | Oral or clinical exposure can engage the proposed pathway | Product, dose, and baseline status can change exposure |
| Pathway | GSK-3 and autophagy signaling is biologically plausible and intervention-relevant | Pathway engagement does not guarantee a clinical benefit |
| Outcomes | Human or translational studies report condition-specific endpoints | No completed trial demonstrates longer life in healthy humans |

Mechanistic evidence is useful for choosing what to measure, not for declaring an outcome in advance.

## 6. Safety and red flags

:::redflag title="Do not confuse availability with safety"
Stop for allergy, persistent gastrointestinal symptoms, neurologic changes, jaundice, unusual bleeding, or any clinically important deterioration.

Evidence in one population does not establish safety in pregnancy, organ impairment, or polypharmacy. Product quality and dose accuracy matter.
:::
