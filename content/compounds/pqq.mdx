> **TL;DR** — PQQ is a redox-cycling molecule that stimulates PGC-1α signaling and mitochondrial biogenesis in cell and animal models — mechanistically in the same territory as urolithin A and CoQ10, but with a much thinner human evidence base: small early trials suggest modest cognitive and inflammatory-marker effects. Tier C; a genuinely interesting mechanistic story that has not yet been confirmed at the scale urolithin A or CoQ10 have.

## 1. What PQQ does

Pyrroloquinoline quinone (PQQ) is a small redox-active compound found in trace amounts in fermented foods, natto, and some produce, that has drawn interest for mitochondrial biology: in cell and animal studies, it activates signaling that increases **PGC-1α** expression — the master regulator of mitochondrial biogenesis — and it can undergo thousands of redox cycles without being consumed, unlike many antioxidants. That mechanistic profile places it in the same conceptual neighborhood as urolithin A (mitophagy) and CoQ10 (electron transport), but PQQ's human trial base is considerably smaller and earlier-stage than either.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** [Mitochondrial dysfunction](/library/mitochondrial-dysfunction) (PGC-1α / biogenesis)

:::evidence tier="C"
Cell and animal studies show PQQ increases PGC-1α expression and mitochondrial biogenesis markers, and it has favorable redox-cycling chemistry. Human evidence is limited to a small number of early trials reporting modest improvements in some cognitive and inflammatory-marker measures — genuinely interesting, but nowhere near the scale, replication, or trial quality of urolithin A's Phase 2 RCT data or CoQ10's larger meta-analyses. Tier C: real mechanistic plausibility, thin human confirmation.
:::

:::practical title="An adjunct experiment, not a replacement for urolithin A or CoQ10"
If mitochondrial biogenesis/quality is your goal, [urolithin A](/library/compounds/urolithina) (mitophagy, Phase 2 RCT evidence) and [CoQ10](/library/compounds/coq10) (electron transport, larger meta-analyses) have substantially stronger human evidence. PQQ is a reasonable, low-risk *addition* to that foundation if you want to experiment with the PGC-1α angle specifically, not a substitute for either.
:::

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Dose:** 10–20 mg/day, matching the range used in most small human trials.
- **Timing:** with food.
- **Duration:** 8–12 weeks to judge subjective cognitive/energy effects, understanding the evidence for any specific endpoint is still early.

:::decision title="Should you add PQQ?"

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Well tolerated** in the small trials conducted to date; no major safety signals reported at typical doses.
- **Limited long-term human safety data** — the evidence base is small, so treat this as a genuinely exploratory addition.

:::redflag title="Treat as exploratory"
- **Limited long-term safety data** — given the small human evidence base, prefer shorter, monitored trial periods
- **Pregnancy or breastfeeding** — insufficient data; avoid
:::
