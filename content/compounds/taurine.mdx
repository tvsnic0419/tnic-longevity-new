> **TL;DR** — Taurine is a sulfur-containing amino acid that buffers mitochondrial and membrane stress; blood levels fall sharply with age. Tier B: Singh 2023 *Science* linked deficiency to aging hallmarks and extended lifespan in mice. 1–3 g/day; retest subjective energy and hs-CRP at week 12.

## 1. What taurine does (and why it matters)

Taurine concentrations in human blood decline roughly 80% between youth and old age. Singh et al. (PMID 37289866) showed taurine supplementation reversed multiple aging markers and extended lifespan across species — one of the strongest recent animal longevity signals for a simple OTC amino acid.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** Mitochondrial dysfunction · Genomic instability · Chronic inflammation

:::evidence tier="B"
PMID 37289866 — Taurine deficiency as a driver of aging (*Science*, 2023). Strong animal lifespan and multi-hallmark data; human longevity endpoints not yet established in large RCTs.
:::

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study | Design | Key outcomes | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Singh 2023 | Multi-species + human association | Lifespan extension mice; taurine correlates with healthspan | B |

**Consensus:** Mechanistically compelling and safe at food-supplement doses; treat as Tier B adjunct, not stack foundation until human longevity RCTs publish.

:::warning title="Where taurine disappoints"
- **The famous result is mouse lifespan plus human *association*, not a human trial.** The 2023 *Science* paper is a strong signal — but extending it to "taurine will make people live longer" is extrapolation the data doesn't yet support.
- **Well-fed omnivores may already be replete.** If you eat meat and seafood regularly, adding taurine may do little; the deficiency story is most relevant to low intake and advanced age.
- **The functional payoff is subtle.** Energy and recovery changes, when they occur, are modest and easy to confuse with placebo. Many people feel nothing and that is a legitimate outcome, not a dosing error.
:::

**What would change this grade.** Taurine is **B** because the mechanism and cross-species data are strong but human outcome trials are missing. It would rise to A with an adequately powered human RCT showing a hard aging or disease endpoint. It would fall if such trials show the striking animal lifespan effect fails to translate to people.

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Start:** 1 g/day with food
- **Target:** 1–3 g/day split AM/PM
- **Duration:** 12+ weeks before judging
- **Retest:** Subjective energy log + hs-CRP if available

## 5. Monitoring

Taurine has no validated blood "level" for consumers to chase, so track function and the inflammatory/metabolic markers it plausibly moves.

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Well tolerated:** 1–6 g/day has a strong human safety record in cardiovascular and metabolic studies.
- **Renal clearance:** Taurine is cleared by the kidneys — reduced clearance in CKD warrants physician oversight.
- **Blood pressure:** May potentiate antihypertensive medication; monitor if you already take BP-lowering drugs.

:::redflag title="Do not self-start without clearance"
- **Chronic kidney disease or dialysis** — impaired taurine clearance; physician clearance required
- **Pregnancy or breastfeeding** — insufficient supplemental-dose safety data
- **On antihypertensives** — additive BP lowering; monitor and coordinate with your prescriber
- **Bipolar disorder** — high-dose amino-acid loading should be physician-supervised
:::

:::warning title="Who should skip taurine"
- **Chronic kidney disease or dialysis** — taurine is renally cleared; needs physician oversight.
- **Regular meat-and-seafood eaters expecting a dramatic effect** — you may already be replete; the money is better spent on a Tier-A foundation.
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding** — insufficient supplemental-dose safety data.
:::
