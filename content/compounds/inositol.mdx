> **TL;DR** — Myo-inositol has the strongest human RCT evidence of any compound in this library for a single specific condition: **PCOS** (polycystic ovary syndrome), where it improves insulin sensitivity and ovulatory function, often studied alongside D-chiro-inositol in a 40:1 ratio. Outside PCOS, evidence for general insulin-sensitivity or mood benefit is real but thinner. Tier B; highly relevant for a specific population, an adjunct elsewhere.

## 1. What inositol does

Inositol is a sugar alcohol involved in cell-membrane signaling (as a precursor to signaling molecules downstream of the insulin receptor) and osmoregulation. Its two most-studied forms are **myo-inositol** and **D-chiro-inositol**, which the body normally maintains in a roughly 40:1 ratio — a ratio that becomes disrupted in insulin-resistant states, including PCOS. That disruption is the basis for the compound's best-evidenced clinical use.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** [Disabled macroautophagy](/library/disabled-macroautophagy) (insulin signaling) · [Altered intercellular communication](/library/altered-intercellular-communication)

:::evidence tier="B"
Multiple RCTs and meta-analyses in women with PCOS show myo-inositol (often combined with D-chiro-inositol at a 40:1 ratio) improves insulin sensitivity, restores ovulatory cycles, and improves some metabolic markers — one of the more consistent, condition-specific human evidence bases in this library. Outside PCOS, smaller trials suggest modest benefit for general insulin sensitivity and some anxiety/mood measures, but the evidence there is thinner and less consistent. Tier B overall, reflecting a strong condition-specific signal alongside weaker general-population evidence.
:::

:::practical title="Strongest case: PCOS. Elsewhere: a reasonable adjunct."
If you have PCOS, myo-inositol (with D-chiro-inositol at 40:1) is one of the better-evidenced non-prescription options for improving insulin sensitivity and ovulatory function — genuinely comparable in some trials to metformin for certain endpoints, with a better tolerability profile. Outside PCOS, treat it as a modest adjunct for insulin-sensitivity or mood goals, not a headline intervention.
:::

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **PCOS:** 2–4 g/day myo-inositol, often combined with D-chiro-inositol at a 40:1 ratio, matching most trial protocols.
- **General insulin-sensitivity/mood use:** 2 g/day myo-inositol is a reasonable, low-risk starting point.
- **Duration:** PCOS trials typically ran 3–6 months for ovulatory/metabolic endpoints.

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Very well tolerated** — one of the better safety profiles in this library; mild GI upset at higher doses is the main complaint.
- **Additive glucose-lowering** — monitor if on insulin or other diabetes medications.
- **Pregnancy** — some evidence supports use in gestational-diabetes-risk contexts under physician guidance, but self-directed use in pregnancy should be physician-supervised.

:::redflag title="Coordinate before starting if any apply"
- **On insulin or glucose-lowering medication** — monitor for additive effect
- **Pregnancy** — physician guidance recommended given hormonal/metabolic relevance
:::
