> **TL;DR** — NAC is a cysteine donor — the rate-limiting substrate for glutathione. It's an approved drug (mucolytic; the acetaminophen-overdose antidote), so its pharmacology is well established. For aging, its key lesson is the [GlyNAC](/library/compounds/glynac) finding: raising cysteine with NAC alone is only half the fix — glycine has to come with it. Tier B for longevity use; 600–1200 mg/day.

## 1. What NAC does

N-Acetylcysteine is a stabilized, absorbable form of the amino acid cysteine, which is the **rate-limiting building block of glutathione (GSH)** — the body's central intracellular antioxidant. NAC is a genuine pharmaceutical: it's used clinically to thin mucus and, most famously, as the **antidote for acetaminophen (paracetamol) overdose**, where it rescues the liver by replenishing glutathione. That approved role is precisely why it's interesting for aging — glutathione falls with age, and NAC restores its limiting substrate.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** [Loss of proteostasis](/library/loss-of-proteostasis) (glutathione substrate) · [Chronic inflammation](/library/chronic-inflammation) · [Genomic instability](/library/genomic-instability) (oxidative DNA protection)

:::evidence tier="B"
NAC's pharmacology is Tier A for its approved indications (mucolytic; acetaminophen antidote). For **aging**, the pivotal insight is the GlyNAC RCTs (Kumar, PMID 34129059): restoring glutathione in older adults required **glycine + NAC together** — cysteine repletion alone was insufficient. Tier B for longevity use: strong mechanism and a clear substrate role, but the healthspan effect is best evidenced in the glycine pairing.
:::

## 2. Mechanism

| Pathway | Mechanism | Hallmark link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Glutathione substrate | Supplies cysteine, the limiting amino acid for GSH synthesis | Loss of proteostasis |
| Direct thiol antioxidant | Free thiol scavenges ROS and breaks disulfide bonds (mucolytic action) | Chronic inflammation |
| Oxidative DNA protection | Restored GSH reduces oxidative DNA lesions | Genomic instability |

:::practical title="The NAC-alone trap"
The most useful thing to know about NAC for aging is a limitation: **NAC alone raises cysteine but does not fully restore aging glutathione** — the GlyNAC trials showed you also need glycine. So if glutathione restoration is your goal, take NAC *with* glycine (or a GlyNAC product), not NAC by itself. NAC solo still has value as a thiol antioxidant and for respiratory mucus.
:::

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study type | Model | Key outcome | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Acetaminophen antidote / mucolytic | Human (approved drug) | Established clinical efficacy | A (those indications) |
| GlyNAC (Kumar, PMID 34129059) | Human RCT | Glutathione restored only with glycine + NAC together | B |
| NAC-alone glutathione repletion in aging | Human | Incomplete without glycine | B |

**Consensus:** Tier B for longevity — a well-characterized cysteine donor whose aging benefit is maximized paired with glycine (GlyNAC), not solo.

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Glutathione support:** 600–1200 mg/day NAC, ideally paired with ~3 g glycine (the GlyNAC ratio), or a combined product.
- **Timing:** with or without food; split doses are fine.
- **Duration:** 12+ weeks for glutathione/oxidative endpoints.

:::decision title="How should you use NAC?"

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Well tolerated** at 600–1200 mg/day; GI upset is the main complaint.
- **Nitroglycerin** — NAC can potentiate its vasodilation/hypotension.
- **Asthma** — rare bronchospasm reports with inhaled forms; oral is generally fine but note sulfur sensitivity.
- **Bleeding** — mild antiplatelet potential at high doses; note if on anticoagulants.

:::redflag title="Coordinate before starting if any apply"
- **On nitroglycerin / nitrates** — additive hypotension
- **On anticoagulants** — possible additive antiplatelet effect at high doses
- **Pregnancy** — use only under clinician guidance outside approved medical use
:::
