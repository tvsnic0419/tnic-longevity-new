> **TL;DR** — Fisetin is a flavonoid with the highest senolytic potency of the dietary compounds tested — it selectively clears senescent "zombie" cells that drive inflammaging. Tier B: a Mayo Clinic pilot RCT reduced senescence markers in older adults. Pulse-dose 20 mg/kg on 2 consecutive days per month; track hs-CRP and function, not a daily biomarker.

## 1. What fisetin does (and why it matters)

Senescent cells accumulate with age and secrete a pro-inflammatory cocktail (the SASP) that damages neighboring tissue. Fisetin is a *senolytic* — it selectively triggers apoptosis in these cells while sparing healthy ones. In the Kirkland group's 2018 screen it showed the highest senolytic potency of the flavonoids tested, clearing 25–50% of senescent cells at physiological concentrations. Because it is dosed in monthly pulses rather than daily, fisetin occupies an unusual niche in a longevity stack.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** Cellular senescence · Altered intercellular communication · Chronic inflammation

:::evidence tier="B"
PMID 30279143 — Yousefzadeh et al., *EBioMedicine* (2018). Fisetin is a senotherapeutic that extends health and lifespan in mice. Human pilot: PMID 31760212 (*EBioMedicine*, 2019) — intermittent high-dose fisetin reduced p16INK4A and p21 senescence markers and improved physical function in older adults.
:::

## 2. Mechanism — clearing the zombie cells

Fisetin destabilizes the anti-apoptotic defenses (notably the BCL-2 family survival signaling) that senescent cells rely on to persist. Once cleared, the SASP falls and normal paracrine signaling is restored. The pulse-dose design mirrors the "hit-and-run" logic of clinical senolytics: brief, high exposure to clear the burden, then a recovery window.

| Pathway | Mechanism | Hallmark link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Senolysis | Selective apoptosis of senescent cells | Cellular senescence |
| SASP reduction | Fewer senescent cells → lower inflammatory secretome | Chronic inflammation |
| Signaling reset | Restores healthy paracrine communication | Altered intercellular communication |

:::practical title="When fisetin is worth your money"
Fisetin earns a stack slot when **two or more** apply:

- Age 50+ with elevated inflammatory markers
- Interest in the senolytic axis, which most stacks leave uncovered
- Preference for a low-frequency (monthly) protocol over daily dosing

**Skip or defer** if you are on anticoagulants or have a bleeding disorder without physician oversight — high-dose flavonoids have mild antiplatelet activity.
:::

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Protocol:** Pulse-dose — 20 mg/kg body weight on 2 consecutive days, once per month (the Mayo pilot regimen)
- **Not daily:** Continuous low-dose fisetin lacks the senolytic evidence base; the burst is the point
- **Form:** Standardized fisetin, ideally a bioavailability-enhanced (liposomal or phospholipid) form — raw fisetin absorbs poorly
- **Timing:** With a fat-containing meal on dosing days
- **Duration:** Reassess inflammatory markers and function after 3 monthly cycles

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Low adverse-event profile:** The Mayo pilot reported good tolerance at pulse doses.
- **Antiplatelet activity:** High-dose flavonoids can mildly reduce platelet aggregation — relevant on anticoagulants or before surgery.
- **Bioavailability caveat:** Raw fisetin is poorly absorbed; an enhanced form is needed to approximate trial exposures.

:::redflag title="Do not self-start without clearance"
- **On anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs** — additive bleeding risk
- **Pregnancy or breastfeeding** — no supplemental-dose safety data
- **Active cancer treatment** — senolytic and antioxidant timing should be oncology-coordinated
- **Bleeding disorder or upcoming surgery** — pause and clear with your physician
:::
