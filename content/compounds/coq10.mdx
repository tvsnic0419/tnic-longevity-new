> **TL;DR** — CoQ10 is an obligate electron-transport-chain cofactor whose synthesis falls ~50% by age 60 and is actively depleted by statins. Ubiquinol (the reduced form) absorbs ~3× better than ubiquinone. Tier B for longevity, Tier A for statin-deficiency correction and heart failure. 100–200 mg/day with fat; retest hs-CRP and energy at week 12.

## 1. What CoQ10 does (and why it matters)

Coenzyme Q10 shuttles electrons between Complexes I/II and III of the mitochondrial electron transport chain — no CoQ10, no ATP. Endogenous synthesis peaks in the twenties and declines roughly 50% by age 60. Statins compound the problem: they block the same mevalonate pathway that produces CoQ10, creating an iatrogenic deficiency in millions of patients. CoQ10 is also a lipid-phase antioxidant, protecting membranes and LDL particles from oxidation.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** Mitochondrial dysfunction · Chronic inflammation · Genomic instability

:::evidence tier="B"
PMID 26267690 — Jorat et al., *Pharmacological Research* (2016). Meta-analysis of 17 RCTs found CoQ10 supplementation significantly reduced hs-CRP and IL-6. Heart-failure outcomes: the Q-SYMBIO trial (PMID 25282031) showed 300 mg/day reduced major adverse cardiac events.
:::

## 2. Mechanism — the electron carrier that runs low with age

CoQ10 is the mobile electron carrier of oxidative phosphorylation. When levels fall — through age, statins, or mitochondrial disease — electron flow stalls, ATP output drops, and reactive oxygen species rise. Restoring CoQ10 re-couples the chain; the ubiquinol form is preferred over 40 because declining reductase capacity limits conversion of ubiquinone.

| Pathway | Mechanism | Hallmark link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Electron transport | Carries electrons between Complexes I/II and III | Mitochondrial dysfunction |
| Antioxidant defense | Reduces LDL and membrane lipid peroxidation | Chronic inflammation |
| Statin repletion | Replaces mevalonate-pathway CoQ10 lost to statins | Mitochondrial dysfunction |

:::practical title="When CoQ10 is worth your money"
CoQ10 earns a stack slot when **two or more** apply:

- You take a statin (near-mandatory repletion)
- Age 50+ with fatigue or reduced exercise tolerance
- Elevated hs-CRP or a heart-failure diagnosis under physician care

**Skip or defer** if you are young, statin-free, and asymptomatic — endogenous synthesis is likely adequate.
:::

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study | Design | Key outcomes | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Jorat 2016 (PMID 26267690) | Meta-analysis, 17 RCTs | hs-CRP and IL-6 significantly reduced | A |
| Q-SYMBIO (PMID 25282031) | RCT, heart failure | Major cardiac events reduced at 300 mg/day | A |
| Statin study 2012 (PMID 22395720) | Mechanistic | Statins deplete skeletal-muscle CoQ10 | B |

**Consensus:** Tier A for statin-induced deficiency and heart-failure adjunct therapy; Tier B as a general longevity compound — the inflammation data are solid but hard longevity endpoints are extrapolated.

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Start:** 100 mg ubiquinol/day with a fat-containing meal
- **Target:** 100–200 mg/day (300 mg in heart-failure protocols, physician-directed)
- **Form:** Ubiquinol (reduced) over 40; ubiquinone is acceptable and cheaper under 40
- **Timing:** Always with dietary fat — CoQ10 is fat-soluble and poorly absorbed otherwise
- **Duration:** 12 weeks before judging energy and inflammatory response

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Very well tolerated:** One of the best-established safety profiles among supplements, including at 300 mg/day.
- **Anticoagulant interaction:** CoQ10 is structurally similar to vitamin K and can reduce warfarin's effect.
- **Absorption-limited:** Without dietary fat, much of an oral dose is wasted — form and timing matter more than megadosing.

:::redflag title="Do not self-start without clearance"
- **On warfarin or other vitamin-K-antagonist anticoagulants** — CoQ10 can blunt anticoagulation; INR monitoring required
- **Pregnancy or breastfeeding** — limited supplemental-dose data; physician clearance
- **Chemotherapy** — antioxidant timing can interact with some regimens; coordinate with oncology
:::
