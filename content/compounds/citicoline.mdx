> **TL;DR** — Citicoline (CDP-choline) is a naturally occurring nucleotide that supplies the raw materials for both neuronal membranes and acetylcholine. In a well-run RCT of healthy older adults with age-associated memory impairment, 500 mg/day improved episodic and composite memory over 12 weeks. A well-tolerated, mechanistically sensible cognition play with better human data than most nootropics. 250–500 mg/day.

## 1. What citicoline does (and why it matters)

Two things fail together in the aging brain: the phospholipid membranes that make neurons work, and the cholinergic signaling that supports memory. Citicoline feeds both — it donates cytidine and choline for membrane phosphatidylcholine synthesis and serves as an acetylcholine precursor. That dual role is why it has been studied for age-related memory decline rather than just marketed for it.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** Altered intercellular communication · Loss of proteostasis

## 2. Mechanism — membranes and acetylcholine

| Pathway | Mechanism | Hallmark link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kennedy pathway | Supplies CDP-choline for phosphatidylcholine (neuronal membrane) synthesis | Loss of proteostasis |
| Cholinergic tone | Choline substrate raises acetylcholine availability | Altered intercellular communication |
| Dopaminergic | Increases dopamine receptor density and release in preclinical models | Altered intercellular communication |

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study | Design | N | Duration | Key outcome | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nakazaki 2021 (PMID: 33978188) | RCT, double-blind | 100 | 12 wk | 500 mg/d improved episodic & composite memory vs placebo in AAMI | B |
| Brown 2012 (PMID: 22974472) | RCT, double-blind | 60 | 12 wk | Antidepressant signal; better study retention (dual-diagnosis sample) | C |

**Consensus:** For healthy older adults with subjective memory decline, the strongest single trial is genuinely positive and the mechanism is coherent. Much of the older evidence base comes from stroke and dementia populations, where results are mixed — so the clean healthy-aging story rests mostly on one good study.

:::warning title="Where citicoline disappoints"
- **Acute stroke recovery** — large trials (e.g. ICTUS) were negative; don't extrapolate the memory data to neurorehabilitation.
- **Young, healthy, unimpaired users** — the memory benefit was shown in people *with* age-associated impairment; baseline matters.
- **Instant effects** — this is a 12-week membrane-remodeling play, not a same-day stimulant.
:::

**What would change this grade.** The tier is **B** because one high-quality healthy-aging RCT plus a coherent mechanism outweigh the mixed disease-population data. It would rise with independent replication in age-associated memory impairment, and fall if replications failed to reproduce the memory effect.

## 4. Dosing protocol

| Parameter | Recommendation | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dose | 250–500 mg/day | 500 mg was the effective memory dose |
| Timing | AM or split | Well tolerated either way |
| Duration | 12 weeks before judging | Membrane effects are slow |

## 5. Monitoring

Track subjective memory, focus, and mood; an optional simple cognitive self-test at baseline and 12 weeks gives a personal signal. No routine labs required.

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Well tolerated** — headache or GI upset are the most common, usually mild.

:::redflag title="Do not self-start without clearance"
- **Bipolar disorder / on psychiatric medication** — the dopaminergic and mood effects warrant clinician oversight.
- **Pregnancy / breastfeeding** — insufficient safety data.
:::

:::warning title="Who should skip citicoline"
- **People expecting stroke-rehab benefit** — the trial evidence there is negative.
- **Those on mood-altering psychiatric drugs** without oversight.
:::

**Works against / redundant with:** No well-characterized antagonists at typical doses. Stacking multiple choline donors (alpha-GPC + citicoline) is usually redundant — pick one.
