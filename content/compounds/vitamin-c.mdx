> **TL;DR** — Vitamin C is an essential water-soluble antioxidant and an obligate cofactor for collagen synthesis (enzymatically required, not just supportive). Human evidence is strongest for its structural role — wound healing, collagen crosslinking, immune-cell function — and genuinely modest for the "megadose prevents colds and cancer" claims that made it famous. Tier B; 250–1000 mg/day covers essentially all evidence-backed use cases.

## 1. What vitamin C does — separating the structural role from the megadose mythology

Vitamin C has an unusually clear, well-established biochemical job: it's an obligate enzymatic cofactor for **prolyl and lysyl hydroxylase**, the enzymes that crosslink collagen — without adequate vitamin C, collagen literally cannot be properly synthesized, which is why severe deficiency (scurvy) causes connective-tissue breakdown. That structural role is Tier A-solid. The much larger cultural reputation vitamin C has — megadosing to prevent or cure colds, or as a cancer treatment — rests on considerably weaker evidence than the collagen story, despite being what most people associate with the vitamin.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** [Loss of proteostasis](/library/loss-of-proteostasis) (collagen synthesis) · [Chronic inflammation](/library/chronic-inflammation) (antioxidant, immune function)

:::evidence tier="B"
Vitamin C's requirement for collagen crosslinking is established biochemistry with strong clinical confirmation (scurvy reversal). Cochrane-level reviews of vitamin C for the common cold find that regular supplementation modestly shortens cold duration but does not meaningfully prevent colds in the general population — a real but much smaller effect than the popular "megadose to prevent colds" claim. Antioxidant and immune-cell-function roles are well characterized mechanistically. Tier B overall: strong for its structural/deficiency role, modest for the popular immune-megadose claim.
:::

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study type | Model | Key outcome | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scurvy reversal / collagen synthesis | Human, clinical | Well-established, obligate cofactor role | A |
| Cochrane review, vitamin C and the common cold | Human, meta-analysis | Modest reduction in cold duration; no meaningful prevention in the general population | B |
| High-dose IV vitamin C in cancer contexts | Human, early/mixed | Investigational; not established standard of care | C |

**Consensus:** Tier B overall — Tier A for its structural/deficiency role, considerably weaker for the popular megadose immune/cancer claims.

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **General/collagen-support use:** 250–500 mg/day, ideally paired with collagen peptide intake.
- **During acute illness:** up to 1000 mg/day is reasonable, understanding the effect on duration is modest, not preventive.
- **Avoid:** very high chronic doses (multi-gram) without a specific reason — see safety section for the kidney-stone consideration.

There's no routine consumer blood test for vitamin C status; track function and skin/wound-healing observations.

| Measure | Target | Frequency | Action if off-target |

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Very well tolerated** at 250–1000 mg/day; GI upset (loose stool) is the main dose-limiting effect at higher doses.
- **Kidney stones** — very high chronic doses (multi-gram) increase urinary oxalate and stone risk in susceptible individuals.
- **Iron overload conditions** (e.g., hemochromatosis) — vitamin C enhances iron absorption; caution in iron-overload conditions.

:::redflag title="Coordinate before high-dose use if any apply"
- **History of kidney stones, especially oxalate stones** — avoid very high chronic doses
- **Hemochromatosis or other iron-overload condition** — vitamin C increases iron absorption
:::
