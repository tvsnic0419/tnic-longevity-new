> **TL;DR** — Lion's Mane is a mushroom studied for stimulating **nerve growth factor (NGF)** synthesis in preclinical models, with one small but genuine human RCT (Mori et al., 2009, older adults with mild cognitive impairment) showing improved cognitive scores that reversed after stopping. That's a real, published signal — and also a single small trial that needs replication before it earns more confidence. Tier C; low-risk, reasonable to try, not yet a well-established intervention.

## 1. What Lion's Mane does

Lion's Mane (*Hericium erinaceus*) is a mushroom containing compounds (hericenones and erinacines) that stimulate **nerve growth factor (NGF)** synthesis in cell and animal studies — NGF supports the growth and maintenance of neurons, which is the mechanistic basis for interest in cognitive and neurological applications. The human evidence, while genuinely promising, rests on a small number of trials.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** [Altered intercellular communication](/library/altered-intercellular-communication) (neurotrophic signaling)

:::evidence tier="C"
Mori et al. (*Phytother Res*, 2009) ran a placebo-controlled RCT in older Japanese adults with mild cognitive impairment: the Lion's Mane group showed improved cognitive function scores during the 16-week treatment period, which declined again after supplementation stopped — a pattern consistent with a real, reversible effect, but from one small trial (n=30). NGF-stimulation mechanism is well established in preclinical models; direct human confirmation of that mechanism (not just the downstream cognitive outcome) is limited. Tier C: a genuine, specific published human signal that has not yet been broadly replicated at scale.
:::

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Dose:** 500–1000 mg/day of a mushroom fruiting-body extract (matching the general range used in available human data).
- **Duration:** the Mori trial ran 16 weeks; judge cognitive-effect claims over a similar horizon, understanding the evidence is early.

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Generally well tolerated** in the available trials; mild GI upset is the main reported complaint.
- **Mushroom/fungal allergy** — avoid if known allergic.
- **Limited long-term human safety data** given the small evidence base.

:::redflag title="Coordinate before starting if any apply"
- **Mushroom or fungal allergy** — avoid
- **On anticoagulants** — some mushroom extracts have mild antiplatelet activity; monitor
:::
