> **TL;DR** — Lion's mane (*Hericium erinaceus*) is a culinary mushroom with a genuinely interesting mechanism: its hericenones and erinacines stimulate nerve growth factor (NGF), the signal that keeps neurons healthy and connected. Early human trials point to cognitive and mood benefits; the animal and mechanistic data are strong, the human evidence still emerging. A promising, low-risk cognition play. 500–1,000 mg extract/day.

## 1. What lion's mane does (and why it matters)

Neuronal maintenance and plasticity fade with age, and NGF — the neurotrophin that supports neuron survival and repair — is central to it. Lion's mane is one of the few natural products shown to raise NGF-related signaling, and that mechanism makes it a compelling candidate for supporting cognition and mood as we age. The upside is meaningful; the honest caveat is that human trials are still small.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** Altered intercellular communication · Chronic inflammation

## 2. Mechanism

Hericenones (fruiting body) and erinacines (mycelium) cross into the CNS and stimulate NGF and BDNF synthesis, supporting neurite outgrowth, remyelination, and hippocampal plasticity. It also has anti-inflammatory and antioxidant activity in neural tissue.

## 3. Evidence

A double-blind RCT in older adults with mild cognitive impairment (Mori, 2009) found improved cognitive scores during supplementation that faded after stopping, and small trials report mood and sleep benefits. Robust animal and mechanistic data support the NGF story; larger, longer human trials are what would move this above Tier C.

## 4. Dosing

500–1,000 mg/day of a standardized extract (look for one that specifies hericenone/erinacine content, and ideally combines fruiting body and mycelium). Give it 8–12 weeks.

## 5. Monitoring

Subjective cognition, mood, and sleep; a simple cognitive self-test at baseline and 12 weeks if you want a signal. No routine labs required.

## 6. Safety & red flags

Well tolerated; it is a food. Rare skin/respiratory allergy has been reported. Because it may affect coagulation slightly, note it if you are on anticoagulants.

## 7. Synergies & stack integration

Complements **citicoline** and **L-theanine** in a cognition stack (NGF support + choline + calm focus), and **omega-3** for neuronal membrane health.
