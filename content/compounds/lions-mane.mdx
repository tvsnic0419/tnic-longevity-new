> **TL;DR** — Lion's mane (*Hericium erinaceus*) is a culinary mushroom with a genuinely interesting mechanism: its hericenones and erinacines stimulate nerve growth factor (NGF), the signal that keeps neurons healthy. A small Japanese RCT in older adults with mild cognitive impairment showed improved scores while taking it — that faded after stopping. Promising and low-risk, but the human evidence is still small and short. 500–1,000 mg extract/day.

## 1. What lion's mane does (and why it matters)

Neuronal maintenance and plasticity fade with age, and NGF — the neurotrophin that supports neuron survival and repair — is central to it. Lion's mane is one of the few natural products shown to raise NGF-related signaling, which makes it a compelling candidate for supporting cognition and mood. The upside is real; the honest caveat is that the human trials are small, short, and mostly from one region.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** Altered intercellular communication · Chronic inflammation

## 2. Mechanism — NGF/BDNF and neurite outgrowth

Hericenones (from the fruiting body) and erinacines (from mycelium) can stimulate NGF and BDNF synthesis, supporting neurite outgrowth and hippocampal plasticity, with additional anti-inflammatory activity in neural tissue. Most of this mechanistic weight comes from animal and cell work; the human trials measure downstream cognitive scores rather than NGF directly.

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study | Design | N | Duration | Key outcome | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mori 2009 (PMID: 18844328) | RCT, double-blind | 30 | 16 wk | Improved cognitive scores (HDS-R) at wk 8–16; **faded 4 wk after stopping** | C |
| Saitsu 2019 (PMID: 31413233) | RCT, double-blind | — | 12 wk | Improved MMSE vs placebo | C |

**Consensus:** Two small placebo-controlled trials point the same direction in people with mild impairment, and the mechanism is well-characterized preclinically. But the samples are tiny, the durations short, and the benefit in Mori's trial reversed once supplementation stopped — so read this as an encouraging early signal, not an established effect.

:::warning title="Where lion's mane disappoints"
- **Durability** — the one trial that checked found benefits faded after stopping; it's not a one-and-done fix.
- **Healthy young cognition** — the data are in *impaired* older adults; don't assume a boost in the unimpaired.
- **Dose/extract chaos** — fruiting-body vs mycelium and hericenone/erinacine content vary wildly between products.
:::

**What would change this grade.** The tier is **C** because human evidence, while positive, is sparse and short — this grade reflects mechanism and short-term surrogates, not outcome trials. It would rise with a larger, longer, independent RCT reproducing the cognitive benefit; it would fall if such a trial were null.

## 5. Monitoring

Subjective cognition, mood, and sleep; an optional simple cognitive self-test at baseline and 12 weeks. No routine labs required.

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Well tolerated** — it is a food mushroom.
- **Allergy** — rare skin/respiratory reactions reported.

:::redflag title="Do not self-start without clearance"
- **Anticoagulant therapy** — possible mild effect on coagulation; note it to your clinician.
- **Known mushroom allergy** — avoid.
:::

:::warning title="Who should skip lion's mane"
- **People wanting a proven cognitive treatment** — the evidence isn't there yet.
- **Those with mushroom allergy.**
- **Anyone expecting lasting benefit without continued use.**
:::
