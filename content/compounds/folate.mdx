> **TL;DR** — Folate (vitamin B9) is essential for DNA synthesis and, with B12, homocysteine clearance. The active, "already-converted" form — **5-MTHF (L-methylfolate)** — bypasses the MTHFR enzyme step that a substantial share of people carry a reduced-function gene variant for, making it a more universally usable form than folic acid. Correcting real deficiency is well established; like TMG and B12, lowering homocysteine hasn't been definitively shown to reduce cardiovascular events in large outcome trials. Tier B.

## 1. What folate does — and why 5-MTHF is the more universal form

Folate is required for DNA synthesis and, together with B12, for converting homocysteine back to methionine. Dietary folate and supplemental folic acid both require enzymatic conversion — the final, rate-limiting step uses the **MTHFR enzyme** — to reach the active form your cells actually use (5-methyltetrahydrofolate, or 5-MTHF). A substantial portion of the population carries MTHFR gene variants (particularly C677T) that reduce this enzyme's efficiency, meaning folic acid conversion is less complete for them. Supplementing directly with **5-MTHF** skips that conversion step entirely, making it the more universally effective form regardless of MTHFR genotype.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** [Genomic instability](/library/genomic-instability) (DNA synthesis) · [Epigenetic alterations](/library/epigenetic-alterations) (methylation) · [Chronic inflammation](/library/chronic-inflammation) (homocysteine)

:::evidence tier="B"
Folate deficiency is a well-established cause of macrocytic anemia and neural-tube defects (the basis for mandatory folic acid fortification and pregnancy supplementation guidance) — genuinely strong, Tier A-level evidence for those specific outcomes. For general homocysteine-lowering and cardiovascular risk, the picture mirrors TMG and B12: HOPE-2 (Lonn et al., *NEJM*, 2006) found a folic acid + B6 + B12 combination lowered homocysteine without reducing cardiovascular events. 5-MTHF's MTHFR-independent absorption is well-documented biochemistry. Tier B overall: strong for deficiency/pregnancy-relevant use, honest gap on cardiovascular-outcome proof.
:::

## 2. Mechanism

| Pathway | Mechanism | Hallmark link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DNA synthesis | Required for nucleotide synthesis and cell division | Genomic instability |
| Methylation support | Feeds the same one-carbon metabolism pool as B12/TMG for DNA and histone methylation | Epigenetic alterations |
| Homocysteine clearance | Cofactor (with B12) for methionine synthase | Chronic inflammation |

:::practical title="5-MTHF over folic acid for most people"
Unless you have a specific reason to use folic acid (e.g., matching a national fortification/pregnancy guideline exactly), **5-MTHF (L-methylfolate)** is the more broadly reliable supplemental form — it doesn't depend on your MTHFR enzyme efficiency, which varies substantially between people.
:::

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study type | Model | Key outcome | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Folate-deficiency correction (anemia, neural-tube defect prevention) | Human, clinical + population | Well-established, strong evidence | A (for these specific outcomes) |
| HOPE-2 (Lonn et al., *NEJM* 2006) | Human RCT, folate+B6+B12 combination | Lowered homocysteine, did not reduce cardiovascular events | A (trial quality) / negative for events |
| MTHFR-variant / 5-MTHF absorption studies | Human, biochemical | 5-MTHF bypasses the MTHFR conversion step | B |

**Consensus:** Tier B. Strong for deficiency correction and pregnancy-relevant use; honest gap between biomarker-lowering and cardiovascular-event reduction, same pattern as TMG and B12.

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **General maintenance:** 400–800 mcg/day 5-MTHF.
- **Correcting elevated homocysteine (with B12):** 800 mcg–1 mg/day 5-MTHF paired with B12, retest at 8–12 weeks.
- **Pregnancy:** follow your obstetric provider's specific folate/folic acid guidance — this module is not pregnancy-specific dosing advice.

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Well tolerated** at supplemental doses; very high-dose folic acid (not 5-MTHF) has a documented ability to mask B12 deficiency symptoms — another reason 5-MTHF and pairing with B12 testing is the safer approach.
- **Methotrexate and certain anti-seizure medications** — these work partly by antagonizing folate metabolism; folate supplementation can interact and must be physician-coordinated.
- **No established toxicity** at typical supplemental 5-MTHF doses.

:::redflag title="Coordinate before starting if any apply"
- **On methotrexate** — folate supplementation requires physician coordination, as methotrexate's mechanism depends on folate antagonism
- **On certain anti-seizure medications** — coordinate with your prescriber
:::
