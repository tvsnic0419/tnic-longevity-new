> **TL;DR** — EPA and DHA incorporate into cell membranes and serve as precursors to specialized pro-resolving mediators (resolvins, protectins) that actively terminate inflammation rather than just suppressing it. The REDUCE-IT trial (n=8,179) showed a high-dose purified EPA drug cut major cardiovascular events 25% — the largest hard-endpoint result of any compound on the TNiC panel. That trial used icosapent ethyl at 4 g/day, a prescription drug, not a generic fish-oil supplement; be precise about that distinction when translating the dose. 2–4 g combined EPA+DHA daily with a fat-containing meal; no cycling required.

## 1. What omega-3 (EPA/DHA) does (and why it matters)

Omega-3 fatty acids — eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) — are long-chain polyunsaturated fats that the body cannot synthesize in adequate quantity on its own. Once absorbed, they incorporate directly into cell membrane phospholipids, where they do two distinct jobs: they change membrane fluidity and receptor behavior, and they serve as the raw material for a class of lipid signaling molecules that most other anti-inflammatory compounds don't touch.

That second role is what separates omega-3s from a typical antioxidant or anti-inflammatory supplement. Most compounds on this panel *suppress* inflammatory signaling (blocking NF-κB, quenching free radicals). EPA and DHA instead feed the *resolution* phase — the active, programmed shutdown of an inflammatory response once its job is done. Chronic low-grade inflammation ("inflammaging") is now understood as, in part, a resolution failure rather than a runaway ignition — and that is the specific gap omega-3s fill.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** Chronic inflammation ("inflammaging") · Altered intercellular communication · Telomere attrition — with cardiovascular disease risk reduction as the best-evidenced secondary benefit (see the REDUCE-IT trial, Section 3).

:::evidence tier="A"
Bhatt DL et al., *N Engl J Med* 2019 (PMID 30415628) — the REDUCE-IT trial: 4 g/day icosapent ethyl (a purified, prescription EPA ethyl ester) reduced major adverse cardiovascular events by 25% in statin-treated patients with elevated triglycerides, versus a mineral-oil placebo. This is the single largest hard-endpoint cardiovascular result behind any compound in this library, and it is the reason omega-3 carries an A tier here — but the dose and drug form tested (icosapent ethyl, 4 g/day EPA-only) are not the same product as an over-the-counter fish-oil capsule. See Section 4 for how that distinction should inform your dosing decision.
:::

## 2. Mechanism — membrane incorporation and active resolution

### 2a. Membrane incorporation and eicosanoid competition

When dietary intake of EPA and DHA rises, they progressively displace arachidonic acid (AA, an omega-6 fatty acid) in the phospholipid bilayer of cell membranes, especially in immune cells, platelets, and vascular endothelium. Because AA is the substrate for the most inflammatory eicosanoids, this displacement changes what enzymes like COX-2 and 5-LOX have available to work with.

| Step | Effect | Hallmark link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EPA/DHA membrane incorporation | Displaces arachidonic acid in phospholipids | Chronic inflammation |
| EPA vs AA competition at COX-2 | Produces less-inflammatory prostanoids (3-series vs 2-series) | Chronic inflammation |
| Membrane fluidity change | Alters receptor clustering, ion channel behavior | Altered intercellular communication |
| Reduced platelet-derived thromboxane A2 | Lower platelet aggregation tendency | Cardiovascular risk |

### 2b. Specialized pro-resolving mediators — active resolution, not suppression

EPA and DHA are also the direct enzymatic precursors to **specialized pro-resolving mediators (SPMs)**: resolvins, protectins, and maresins. These are synthesized on demand from membrane-liberated EPA/DHA via lipoxygenase pathways, and they don't simply block inflammatory signaling — they actively drive the resolution program: stopping further immune cell recruitment, promoting macrophage-mediated clearance of debris and dead cells (efferocytosis), and resetting tissue back to a homeostatic baseline.

| SPM family | Precursor | Resolution action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E-series resolvins | EPA | Stops neutrophil recruitment, reduces vascular permeability |
| D-series resolvins | DHA | Promotes macrophage efferocytosis of apoptotic cells |
| Protectins | DHA | Neuroprotective and anti-inflammatory in neural tissue |
| Maresins | DHA | Macrophage-derived, accelerates tissue repair |

:::pathway title="EPA/DHA resolution axis" nodes="EPA/DHA|Dietary/supplement intake|cyan,Cell membranes|Phospholipid incorporation|amber,Eicosanoid shift|Less AA-derived signaling|rose,SPMs|Resolvins/protectins/maresins|violet,Inflammation resolution|Outcome|emerald" edges="EPA/DHA->Cell membranes|incorporates,Cell membranes->Eicosanoid shift|displaces AA,EPA/DHA->SPMs|enzymatic precursor,SPMs->Inflammation resolution|actively resolves,Eicosanoid shift->Inflammation resolution|reduces trigger"
:::

Because this is a substrate-availability mechanism — more EPA/DHA in the membrane means more raw material for SPM synthesis — the effect scales with sustained dietary or supplemental intake rather than acting like a drug with a fixed on/off threshold.

:::practical title="When omega-3 earns a stack slot"
Omega-3 has clear value when **two or more** apply:

- Elevated triglycerides, especially with a cardiovascular risk profile similar to REDUCE-IT's population (statin-treated, elevated TG)
- Chronic inflammation (elevated hs-CRP) is a top hallmark priority
- Dietary EPA/DHA intake is low (limited fatty fish consumption)
- You want a foundational, well-tolerated compound to run continuously alongside more targeted interventions

**Skip or defer:** if you're already on anticoagulant or antiplatelet therapy without physician oversight (additive bleeding risk); if triglycerides are the primary concern and you may be a candidate for prescription icosapent ethyl, discuss that option with a physician before assuming an OTC supplement will replicate REDUCE-IT's results.
:::

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study | Design | Population | Key outcomes | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bhatt 2019 / REDUCE-IT (PMID 30415628) | Large RCT, n=8,179 | Statin-treated adults, elevated triglycerides, high CV risk | ↓25% major adverse cardiovascular events vs mineral-oil placebo | A |
| Kiecolt-Glaser 2013 (PMID 23010452) | Human RCT | Sedentary, overweight middle-aged/older adults | ↓ IL-6/TNF-α inflammatory markers; reduced oxidative stress markers linked to telomere biology | A |

**Consensus:** Tier A is justified specifically by the scale and rigor of REDUCE-IT — a large, multi-year, hard-cardiovascular-endpoint RCT (n=8,179) showing a 25% relative risk reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events, which is a categorically stronger result than the biomarker- or surrogate-endpoint trials most supplements rely on. That result, however, was produced by a specific prescription drug (icosapent ethyl, purified EPA, 4 g/day) — not a generic over-the-counter fish-oil blend of mixed EPA+DHA, and not necessarily at OTC-typical doses. Readers should not assume a standard fish-oil capsule at 1–2 g/day will reproduce REDUCE-IT's cardiovascular outcome; the mechanistic rationale (membrane incorporation, SPM production, eicosanoid competition) applies to EPA/DHA broadly, but the specific magnitude of cardiovascular benefit is tied to that trial's drug and dose. The Kiecolt-Glaser 2013 RCT independently supports an anti-inflammatory and oxidative-stress-reduction effect from omega-3 supplementation in a general (non-cardiac) population, which is the more directly relevant evidence for standard supplemental use.

**Fish oil vs icosapent ethyl vs krill/algae forms:** Triglyceride (re-esterified) fish oil has better absorption than standard ethyl-ester fish oil. Icosapent ethyl (Vascepa) is a prescription-only, EPA-only ethyl ester used at doses well above typical OTC labels. Algae-derived DHA/EPA is a vegan-compatible alternative with comparable bioavailability to fish-derived triglyceride forms.

## 4. Dosing protocol

| Parameter | Recommendation | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dose | 2–4 g combined EPA+DHA daily | Higher end (near 4 g) approaches REDUCE-IT's EPA-only dose but as a mixed EPA+DHA OTC supplement, not the same drug |
| Form | Triglyceride (re-esterified) fish oil, krill oil, or algae-derived EPA/DHA | Triglyceride form has better absorption than standard ethyl-ester fish oil; algae form is vegan-compatible |
| Timing | AM/PM, with a fat-containing meal | Fat co-ingestion meaningfully improves absorption of the fat-soluble EPA/DHA |
| Duration | 8–12 weeks minimum to assess triglyceride/hs-CRP response | REDUCE-IT ran multiple years for cardiovascular endpoints; inflammatory biomarker changes are seen sooner |
| Cycling | Continuous | No cycling data; sustained membrane incorporation is the mechanism, so continuous use is the studied protocol |

### AM/PM split-dose ramp-in schedule

| Week | Time | Dose | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–2 | AM with breakfast | 1 g EPA+DHA | Confirm GI tolerance (fishy aftertaste, mild reflux) before increasing |
| 3–4 | AM + PM with meals | 1 g AM + 1 g PM (2 g total) | Split dosing with food reduces GI symptoms and improves absorption |
| 5+ | AM + PM with meals | 1.5–2 g AM + 1.5–2 g PM (3–4 g total) | Only escalate toward the upper end if triglycerides remain elevated at reassessment |

:::practical title="Titration and quality checklist"
- [ ] Start at 1 g/day for 1–2 weeks to confirm GI tolerance before splitting into AM/PM dosing
- [ ] Always take with a fat-containing meal — absorption drops substantially in the fasted state
- [ ] Choose triglyceride-form or algae-derived EPA/DHA over standard ethyl-ester for better absorption
- [ ] Check third-party testing (oxidation/TOTOX value, heavy metals) — fish oil degrades and oxidizes over time
- [ ] Log triglycerides and hs-CRP at baseline and 8–12 weeks
- [ ] If targeting a strong triglyceride-lowering effect near REDUCE-IT's magnitude, discuss prescription icosapent ethyl with a physician rather than escalating an OTC blend indefinitely
:::

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Generally very well tolerated** at 1–4 g combined EPA+DHA in human studies, including REDUCE-IT's multi-year duration
- **Mild GI symptoms** — fishy aftertaste, belching, mild reflux; taking with meals and choosing enteric-coated or triglyceride-form products reduces this
- **Bleeding risk** — omega-3s have a mild antiplatelet effect; this is additive with anticoagulant/antiplatelet medications and relevant before surgery
- **Atrial fibrillation signal** — REDUCE-IT and related high-dose EPA trials observed a higher incidence of atrial fibrillation in the treatment arm versus placebo; this is a real, trial-documented finding at pharmaceutical doses and should not be dismissed

:::redflag title="Do not self-start without physician review"
- **Anticoagulant or antiplatelet medications (warfarin, DOACs, aspirin, clopidogrel)** — additive bleeding risk; physician oversight required before combining
- **Upcoming surgery** — discontinue per your surgeon's guidance given the antiplatelet effect
- **History of atrial fibrillation** — high-dose EPA (as tested in REDUCE-IT) was associated with increased AFib incidence; discuss dose and monitoring with a physician
- **Fish or shellfish allergy** — use algae-derived EPA/DHA instead of fish- or krill-derived forms
:::

:::warning title="Soft cautions"
Fishy aftertaste, belching, and mild GI upset are the most common complaints, especially at higher doses or with lower-quality (oxidized) products. Taking with meals, choosing triglyceride-form or enteric-coated products, and keeping the product refrigerated after opening resolves most cases.
:::
