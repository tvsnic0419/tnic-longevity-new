> **TL;DR** — Rhodiola rosea is an adaptogenic herb with human RCT evidence for reducing fatigue and improving mental performance under stress, particularly in burnout and stress-related fatigue populations. The evidence base is smaller and more heterogeneous (varied extracts, doses, and standardizations) than ashwagandha's. Tier B; 200–400 mg/day of a standardized extract (≥3% rosavins, ≥1% salidroside).

## 1. What Rhodiola does

Rhodiola rosea is an adaptogenic herb, traditionally used in Russian and Scandinavian folk medicine, studied mainly for **anti-fatigue** and **stress-resilience** effects. Its proposed active compounds are rosavins and salidroside, and clinical trials have generally used extracts standardized to these markers. Compared to ashwagandha, rhodiola's evidence base is real but more heterogeneous — different studies used different extracts, doses, and standardizations, making cross-trial comparison harder.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** [Altered intercellular communication](/library/altered-intercellular-communication) (stress response, fatigue) · [Mitochondrial dysfunction](/library/mitochondrial-dysfunction) (energy/fatigue)

:::evidence tier="B"
Several placebo-controlled human RCTs report reduced fatigue and improved mental-performance measures (attention, reduced errors) under sustained stress or workload, particularly in populations with stress-related fatigue or mild burnout symptoms. A meta-analysis-level view shows generally positive but heterogeneous effects across differing extracts and doses. Tier B: real human RCT signal, with more study-to-study variability than the library's higher-confidence compounds.
:::

:::practical title="Check the standardization"
Look for extracts standardized to **≥3% rosavins and ≥1% salidroside** (roughly a 3:1 ratio), matching the extract used in most positive trials. Unstandardized "rhodiola root" products vary widely in actual active-compound content.
:::

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study type | Model | Key outcome | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standardized-extract RCTs (fatigue) | Human, stress/burnout populations | Reduced perceived fatigue | B |
| Standardized-extract RCTs (cognitive performance) | Human, under sustained workload | Improved attention/error-rate measures | B |

**Consensus:** Tier B. Real, if heterogeneous, human RCT evidence — most reliable in stress/fatigue-specific populations rather than as a general-purpose energy compound.

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Dose:** 200–400 mg/day of a standardized extract (≥3% rosavins, ≥1% salidroside).
- **Timing:** morning, as it can have a mild activating effect.
- **Duration:** 4–8 weeks to judge fatigue/performance effects.

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Generally well tolerated**; mild jitteriness or sleep disruption possible if taken late in the day.
- **Bipolar disorder** — activating adaptogens warrant caution; discuss with a physician.
- **Stimulant interaction** — possible additive activating effect with caffeine or prescription stimulants.

:::redflag title="Coordinate before starting if any apply"
- **Bipolar disorder** — physician oversight given activating properties
- **On stimulant medication** — possible additive effect
:::
