> **TL;DR** — Serrapeptase is a proteolytic enzyme originally from Serratia bacteria in silkworm gut, marketed for pain, sinus congestion, and "fibrinolytic" effects. Human RCT evidence is small and heterogeneous — real but modest signals in post-surgical inflammation (Bhagat 2013 PMID 24399011) and breast engorgement (Kee 1989); marketed longevity/cardiovascular claims outrun the data. Enteric-coated 10,000–60,000 SU/day between meals. Not a substitute for standard therapy — treat with caution.

## 1. What serrapeptase does (and why it matters)

Serrapeptase (serratiopeptidase, silk worm enzyme) is a bacterial protease that hydrolyzes non-living proteins — fibrin clots, mucus, cellular debris. In Japan and Europe it has been used for decades post-surgically for inflammation and edema; the FDA has never approved it as a drug.

Its "fibrinolytic" and "anti-inflammatory" properties depend on the enzyme actually reaching the systemic circulation intact — which requires enteric coating (survives stomach acid) and only modest absorption at best. Human PK data for enteric serrapeptase is weak; the mechanism between "you take a pill" and "systemic effect" is contentious.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** Chronic inflammation (post-injury / post-surgical) · Altered intercellular communication (fibrin turnover)

:::evidence tier="C"
Bhagat et al. (2013, PMID 24399011) — 30 dental impaction extraction patients — serrapeptase 30 mg TID × 5 days reduced post-op swelling and trismus vs placebo. Mazzone et al. (1990) small ENT trials showed benefit in acute/chronic sinusitis symptom scores. Kee (1989) — 70 postpartum women — serrapeptase reduced breast engorgement swelling. **However**, larger meta-analyses find effect sizes small and study quality generally low. No RCT-quality evidence supports the marketed "atherosclerotic plaque dissolution" claims.
:::

## 2. Mechanism

| Pathway | Mechanism | Hallmark link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fibrinolysis (proposed) | Enzymatic cleavage of fibrin in blood clots | Communication |
| Mucolysis | Reduces mucus viscosity in sinus/respiratory | — |
| Bradykinin degradation | Reduces inflammatory mediator pool | Inflammation |
| Anti-edema | Improves lymphatic drainage of exudate | Inflammation |
| Post-surgical recovery | ↓ Swelling, trismus, edema | — |

The **oral bioavailability question is unresolved**. Some in vitro and small human PK studies suggest partial intact absorption; others find it fully digested. This uncertainty is why serrapeptase remains on the fringes of evidence-based practice.

:::practical title="When serrapeptase is worth trying"
Serrapeptase is worth thought only in narrow cases:

- Post-surgical / post-dental extraction inflammation (dental literature is the strongest)
- Chronic sinusitis with thick mucus refractory to standard therapy
- Post-mastitis or breast engorgement

**Skip or defer** for the marketed "cardiovascular / anti-atherosclerotic" claims — that evidence does not exist at RCT quality. Do not use as a substitute for anticoagulation.
:::

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study | Design | N | Duration | Key outcomes | Tier |

| Bhagat 2013 (PMID 24399011) | RCT (dental) | 30 | 5 d | ↓ Post-op swelling, trismus | C |
| Kee 1989 | RCT (mastitis) | 70 | 3 d | ↓ Breast engorgement | C |
| Mazzone 1990 | RCT (ENT) | 195 | 8 d | ↓ Sinusitis symptoms | C |
| Marketed cardiovascular claims | — | — | — | **No RCT evidence** | — |

**Consensus:** Tier C. Real but small effects in specific inflammatory contexts. Marketed longevity/cardiovascular claims are unsupported by RCT evidence.

:::warning title="Where serrapeptase disappoints"
- **The "arterial plaque dissolver" marketing is not evidence-based.** No RCT shows serrapeptase reduces coronary or carotid plaque burden.
- **PK is contentious.** Whether oral serrapeptase reaches meaningful systemic levels is unproven.
- **Enteric coating matters.** Non-enteric serrapeptase is destroyed by stomach acid; product quality is highly variable.
- **The Japanese oncology / cardiovascular use** predates modern evidence standards.
:::

## 4. Dosing protocol

| Parameter | Recommendation | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enteric-coated tablets | 10,000–60,000 SU (units) daily | 60,000 SU = ~120 mg enzyme |
| Timing | Between meals (empty stomach) | Food destroys uncoated enzyme |
| Duration | 5–14 days for post-surgical use | Not for chronic use without evaluation |

## 5. Monitoring

| Biomarker | Target | Frequency | Action if off-target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Post-surgical swelling | Reducing | Daily first week | Standard care remains primary |
| Bleeding signs | None | Ongoing | Discontinue immediately if noted |
| Sinusitis symptoms | Improving | Weekly | Combine with saline irrigation |

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Well tolerated** at typical doses; mild GI upset uncommon.
- **Theoretical bleeding risk** — proteolytic enzyme could interact with anticoagulants.
- **Rare pulmonary infiltrates** reported in Japanese post-marketing.

:::redflag title="Do not self-start without clearance"
- **On warfarin, DOACs, antiplatelets** — theoretical additive bleeding effect.
- **Active bleeding or ulcer disease** — proteolytic activity may worsen.
- **Scheduled surgery** — discontinue 2 weeks before.
- **Pregnancy** — insufficient safety data.
- **Do not use as anticoagulant substitute** for atrial fibrillation, DVT, or established cardiovascular disease.
:::
