> **TL;DR** — Vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol) is a hormone precursor, not just a vitamin — it regulates calcium, bone, immune, and inflammatory signaling through the vitamin D receptor (VDR) in nearly every tissue. The landmark VITAL trial (Manson et al., *NEJM*, 2019, n=25,871) found no reduction in cancer or cardiovascular events for unselected adults — a genuinely important null result — but did find fewer autoimmune-disease diagnoses and a signal for reduced cancer *mortality*. Deficiency correction (not megadosing) is the actual evidence-backed target. Tier B; dose to a blood level, not a fixed number.

## 1. What vitamin D3 does — and the honest VITAL-trial reset

Vitamin D3 is synthesized in skin from UVB exposure and converted in the liver and kidney to its active hormone form, which binds the vitamin D receptor (VDR) — expressed in immune cells, bone, muscle, and most other tissues — to regulate hundreds of genes. For years, low vitamin D was correlated with nearly every disease of aging, which fueled enormous supplementation enthusiasm. The VITAL trial (Manson et al., *N Engl J Med*, 2019) is the single most important reset to that story: 2000 IU/day in adults **not selected for deficiency** did not reduce overall cancer incidence or major cardiovascular events. It did find fewer new autoimmune-disease diagnoses and — in secondary/pooled analyses — a reduction in cancer *mortality* despite no change in incidence. The honest takeaway: vitamin D correction matters most for people who are actually deficient, and un-targeted megadosing in replete adults has not shown the broad benefit the correlational data implied.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** [Chronic inflammation](/library/chronic-inflammation) (immune/VDR signaling) · [Altered intercellular communication](/library/altered-intercellular-communication) (endocrine/hormone axis) · [Stem cell exhaustion](/library/stem-cell-exhaustion) (bone/muscle maintenance)

:::evidence tier="B"
VITAL (Manson et al., *NEJM* 2019) is the largest, best-designed vitamin D RCT: no reduction in overall cancer incidence or major cardiovascular events in an unselected population, but fewer autoimmune-disease diagnoses and a cancer-mortality signal. Observational data consistently links low 25(OH)D to worse outcomes across many conditions, but correlation ≠ correction-causation, which VITAL partly confirmed and partly refuted. Tier B: genuinely useful for correcting real deficiency; not established as a broad-benefit megadose intervention in replete adults.
:::

## 2. Mechanism

| Pathway | Mechanism | Hallmark link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VDR gene regulation | Binds vitamin D receptor across immune, bone, and muscle tissue | Communication |
| Immune modulation | Reduced new autoimmune-disease diagnoses in VITAL | Chronic inflammation |
| Bone / calcium homeostasis | Regulates intestinal calcium absorption and bone mineralization | Stem cell exhaustion |
| Muscle function | VDR expressed in skeletal muscle; deficiency linked to weakness/falls | Stem cell exhaustion |

:::practical title="Test, don't guess"
Vitamin D is the clearest case in this library for **testing before dosing**. VITAL's null result for cancer/CVD was in an unselected (largely replete) population — the people most likely to benefit are those with a measured deficiency. Get a 25(OH)D level, correct to a normal range if low, and don't assume "more is better."
:::

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study | Design | Key outcomes | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| VITAL (Manson et al., *NEJM* 2019, n=25,871) | Human RCT, unselected adults | No reduction in overall cancer/CVD incidence; fewer autoimmune diagnoses; cancer-mortality signal | A (trial quality) / B (net benefit unclear for replete adults) |
| Deficiency-correction literature | Observational + smaller RCTs | Bone, muscle, and mood benefits concentrated in deficient individuals | B |

**Consensus:** Tier B. Correcting a real deficiency is well supported; megadosing replete adults for broad disease prevention is not, per VITAL.

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Test first:** 25(OH)D blood level before starting a protocol.
- **Deficient (<20 ng/mL):** typically 4000–5000 IU/day short-term to correct, then retest at 8–12 weeks.
- **Maintenance (sufficient):** 1000–2000 IU/day is a reasonable maintenance dose for most adults with limited sun exposure.
- **Take with fat-containing food** — D3 is fat-soluble.

:::decision title="Do you need vitamin D3?"
node | Do you have a measured 25(OH)D level below ~30 ng/mL?
yes | Correct with 4000–5000 IU/day, retest at 8–12 weeks, then drop to maintenance
no | node | Is your level already sufficient (30+ ng/mL)?
  yes | A modest maintenance dose (1000–2000 IU/day) is reasonable; megadosing has not shown added benefit
  no | Get a level tested before deciding — this is a test-first compound
redflag | Hypercalcemia, sarcoidosis, or other granulomatous disease → physician oversight; vitamin D can worsen calcium handling
:::

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Well tolerated** within recommended ranges; toxicity is rare and dose-dependent at sustained very-high intake.
- **Hypercalcemia risk** at high sustained doses, especially with granulomatous disease (e.g., sarcoidosis) or primary hyperparathyroidism.
- **Kidney stones** — some evidence of increased risk with high-dose D + calcium co-supplementation.
- **Drug interactions** — thiazide diuretics can raise calcium alongside vitamin D; coordinate if on one.

:::redflag title="Coordinate before high-dose correction if any apply"
- **Sarcoidosis or other granulomatous disease** — abnormal vitamin D sensitivity
- **History of kidney stones or hypercalcemia** — physician oversight
- **On thiazide diuretics** — additive calcium-elevation risk
:::
