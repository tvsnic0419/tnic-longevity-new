> **TL;DR** — HMB is a leucine metabolite with real human RCT evidence for preserving muscle mass specifically in **older or clinically vulnerable populations** — bed rest, illness recovery, and sarcopenia-risk contexts — where it appears more clearly useful than in young, healthy, well-trained lifters (where creatine and adequate protein already dominate). Tier B; a genuine anti-catabolic tool for the specific populations most at risk of muscle loss.

## 1. What HMB does

HMB is a metabolite of the amino acid leucine, and its proposed mechanism is distinct from most muscle-support compounds in this library: rather than primarily driving muscle protein *synthesis*, HMB is thought to reduce muscle protein *breakdown* (an anti-catabolic effect) via effects on the ubiquitin-proteasome degradation pathway. That mechanistic angle is why its clearest human evidence comes from populations experiencing active muscle-wasting stress — bed rest, illness, and older adults at risk of sarcopenia — rather than from young, healthy, resistance-trained athletes, where the existing literature is more mixed.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** [Stem cell exhaustion](/library/stem-cell-exhaustion) (sarcopenia, muscle-wasting prevention) · [Loss of proteostasis](/library/loss-of-proteostasis) (protein degradation pathway)

:::evidence tier="B"
RCTs in older adults and in bed-rest/immobilization models show HMB (typically 3 g/day) reduces muscle-mass and strength loss compared to placebo — a meaningfully useful anti-catabolic signal in populations actively at risk of losing muscle. In young, healthy, resistance-trained individuals, the evidence for added benefit beyond adequate protein intake and training is considerably weaker and more mixed across trials. Tier B: real, population-dependent evidence — strongest exactly where a longevity audience needs it most (older adults, illness/injury recovery), weaker where most sports-nutrition marketing targets it.
:::

## 2. Mechanism

| Pathway | Mechanism | Hallmark link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anti-catabolic (ubiquitin-proteasome) | Proposed reduction in muscle protein breakdown signaling | Loss of proteostasis |
| Sarcopenia prevention | Preserved muscle mass/strength in older-adult and bed-rest RCTs | Stem cell exhaustion |

:::practical title="Most useful where muscle loss is actively happening"
HMB's clearest value is in situations of active or elevated muscle-wasting risk: recovering from illness or surgery, a period of forced bed rest/immobilization, or general sarcopenia risk in older age. For a healthy, actively resistance-training adult already getting adequate protein, [creatine](/library/compounds/creatine) has the stronger, more general evidence base.
:::

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study type | Model | Key outcome | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Older-adult / sarcopenia RCTs | Human | Reduced muscle mass/strength loss vs placebo | B |
| Bed-rest / immobilization RCTs | Human | Attenuated muscle loss during forced inactivity | B |
| Young, healthy, resistance-trained populations | Human | Mixed, often no significant added benefit over training + protein alone | C |

**Consensus:** Tier B overall, reflecting genuinely useful population-specific evidence (older adults, illness/injury recovery) alongside weaker evidence in healthy young trainees.

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Dose:** 3 g/day, matching most positive trials (often split into 3x 1g doses with meals).
- **Best-evidenced context:** older adults, periods of bed rest/immobilization, or illness/injury recovery — situations of active muscle-loss risk.
- **Duration:** trial benefits were measured over weeks to a few months, matching the recovery/at-risk period.

:::decision title="Should you take HMB?"
node | Are you older, recovering from illness/surgery, or facing a period of forced inactivity?
yes | 3 g/day is a reasonable, well-evidenced anti-catabolic addition for this specific situation
no | node | Are you a healthy, actively resistance-training adult with adequate protein intake?
  yes | Creatine has stronger, more general evidence for this population — prioritize it first
  no | Lower priority

:::

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Excellent safety record** — HMB has one of the cleaner safety profiles in this library; no significant adverse effects reported at studied doses.
- **No significant drug interactions** established.

:::redflag title="No major established contraindications at typical doses"
- **Pregnancy or breastfeeding** — limited supplemental-dose safety data; use only under clinician guidance
:::
