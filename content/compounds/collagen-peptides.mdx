> **TL;DR** — Hydrolyzed collagen peptides are one of the best-evidenced supplements for the visible and structural side of aging: human RCTs show improved skin elasticity and hydration, reduced joint pain, and support for bone density. The peptides act as both building blocks and signals that tell fibroblasts and chondrocytes to build. 10–15 g/day.

## 1. What collagen peptides do (and why it matters)

Collagen is the scaffolding of skin, tendon, cartilage, and bone, and its synthesis falls with age — which shows up as thinner skin, achier joints, and weaker bone. Hydrolyzed collagen delivers a distinctive pool of glycine, proline, and hydroxyproline plus specific bioactive di- and tri-peptides that survive digestion, reach connective tissue, and stimulate the cells there to build new matrix. The result is a supplement with unusually direct, visible payoffs.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** Altered intercellular communication · Chronic inflammation

## 2. Mechanism

Signature peptides such as prolyl-hydroxyproline reach the dermis and joints and act as signaling fragments, upregulating collagen, elastin, and hyaluronic-acid synthesis by fibroblasts and chondrocytes — not merely supplying amino acids but instructing the repair machinery.

## 3. Evidence

Placebo-controlled RCTs report improved skin elasticity and hydration (Proksch and others), reduced activity-related joint pain (Clark, in athletes), and improved bone mineral density over 12 months (König). Effects build over 8–24 weeks of daily use.

## 4. Dosing

10–15 g/day of hydrolyzed collagen peptides. For skin, specific bioactive collagen peptide formulations are studied at 2.5–5 g; general connective-tissue support uses 10–15 g. Vitamin C supports collagen synthesis — co-ingest.

## 5. Monitoring

Skin: photograph and track hydration/elasticity over 12 weeks. Joints: track pain and function. Bone: DEXA at baseline and 12 months if bone is the goal.

## 6. Safety & red flags

Very safe and well tolerated. Choose a tested source (heavy-metal screened). It is not a complete protein, so it does not replace dietary protein for muscle.

## 7. Synergies & stack integration

Pair with **vitamin C** (collagen cofactor) and **vitamin K2** for bone. Complements resistance training and adequate dietary protein; sits alongside **hyaluronic acid** in a skin/joint matrix stack.
