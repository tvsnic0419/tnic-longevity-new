> **TL;DR** — EGCG (epigallocatechin gallate) is the primary catechin in green tea, with real human data for metabolic and cardiovascular markers and mechanistic AMPK-activation overlap with berberine/metformin. The essential caveat most marketing skips: concentrated **supplemental** EGCG (not brewed tea) carries a genuine, regulator-flagged **hepatotoxicity risk** at high doses, especially taken fasted. Tier B; dose conservatively, with food, and prefer brewed tea or moderate-dose extracts over high-dose isolated EGCG.

## 1. What EGCG does — and the dose that matters

EGCG is the most abundant and most-studied catechin polyphenol in green tea. It has genuine, dose-dependent biological activity: it activates AMPK (the same energy-sensing pathway berberine and metformin engage), modulates inflammatory signaling, and has antioxidant activity. The complication is that green tea's long safety record comes from **brewed tea**, which delivers EGCG gradually alongside other tea compounds — concentrated supplemental extracts deliver a much larger, faster bolus dose, and that form is where liver-toxicity signals have appeared.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** [Disabled macroautophagy](/library/disabled-macroautophagy) (AMPK activation) · [Chronic inflammation](/library/chronic-inflammation) · [Mitochondrial dysfunction](/library/mitochondrial-dysfunction)

:::evidence tier="B"
Human trials of brewed green tea and moderate-dose EGCG extracts show modest improvements in LDL cholesterol, blood pressure, and some glycemic markers, plus AMPK-activation mechanistic data overlapping with berberine/metformin. Separately, regulatory bodies (EFSA in Europe; case reports reviewed by USP and hepatology literature) have flagged **concentrated EGCG extracts at high doses (roughly ≥800 mg/day EGCG, especially fasted)** with rare but real cases of liver injury. Tier B: genuine metabolic benefit at sane doses, with an honest, regulator-acknowledged safety ceiling that most supplement marketing omits.
:::

| Pathway | Mechanism | Hallmark link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AMPK activation | Overlaps with berberine/metformin's energy-sensing pathway | Disabled macroautophagy |
| Lipid metabolism | Modest LDL and blood-pressure reductions in trials | Chronic inflammation |
| Antioxidant / anti-inflammatory | Polyphenol radical-scavenging and signaling effects | Mitochondrial dysfunction |
| Hepatic bolus-dose risk | Concentrated extracts at high, fasted doses linked to rare liver injury | — (safety-limiting) |

:::practical title="Prefer brewed tea or moderate-dose extracts, with food"
The safest way to get EGCG's benefit is **brewed green tea** (2–4 cups/day) or a **moderate-dose extract taken with food**, not a high-dose fasted-state concentrate. If using an extract, stay meaningfully below the doses associated with liver-injury case reports and avoid fasted dosing.
:::

**Consensus:** Tier B for metabolic benefit at sane doses. The hepatotoxicity signal at high concentrated doses is real and regulator-acknowledged — respect the dose ceiling.

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Preferred:** brewed green tea, 2–4 cups/day.
- **If using an extract:** stay at or below ~300–400 mg EGCG/day, taken **with food**, not fasted.
- **Avoid:** high-dose isolated EGCG extracts (≥800 mg/day), especially on an empty stomach.
- **Duration:** judge metabolic markers at 12 weeks.

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Brewed tea:** excellent long-term safety record.
- **Concentrated extracts:** rare but real hepatotoxicity reports at high, often fasted, doses — this is an EFSA-acknowledged safety signal, not internet speculation.
- **Iron absorption** — tea polyphenols can reduce non-heme iron absorption; space away from iron-rich meals/supplements if iron status is a concern.
- **Caffeine content** — most EGCG extracts and brewed tea contain caffeine; account for total intake.

:::redflag title="Do not exceed conservative doses of concentrated extracts"
- **Existing liver disease** — avoid concentrated EGCG extracts
- **On other hepatotoxic medications** — avoid stacking risk; clear with a physician
- **Fasted high-dose extract use** — the specific pattern most associated with liver-injury case reports; never do this
:::
