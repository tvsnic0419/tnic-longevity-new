> **TL;DR** — Cocoa flavanols (notably epicatechin) improve endothelial function and modestly lower blood pressure, and the large COSMOS trial hinted at a cardiovascular-death benefit — but it *missed* its primary total-CVD endpoint, and the companion cognition trial found no memory benefit at all. So: a legitimate vascular support with honest limits, not a brain supplement. ~500 mg flavanols/day.

## 1. What cocoa flavanols do (and why it matters)

Endothelial function — the artery lining's ability to dilate — declines with age and predicts cardiovascular risk. Cocoa flavanols raise nitric-oxide bioavailability and improve that dilation, with small reductions in blood pressure. The honest framing is that this is a vascular-tone story with a mixed hard-outcome trial behind it, and specifically *not* a cognitive one.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** Altered intercellular communication · Chronic inflammation

## 2. Mechanism — epicatechin, eNOS, and nitric oxide

Flavanol monomers (chiefly (−)-epicatechin) upregulate endothelial nitric-oxide synthase (eNOS) and raise NO bioavailability, improving flow-mediated dilation and lowering peripheral resistance. Secondary antioxidant and anti-inflammatory actions may contribute, but the NO/endothelial axis is the best-supported route.

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study | Design | N | Duration | Key outcome | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sesso 2022 — COSMOS (PMID: 35294962) | RCT | 21,442 | 3.6 y | Primary total-CVD **not** significant (HR 0.90, P=0.11); CVD death −27% (secondary) | B |
| Baker 2022 — COSMOS-Mind (PMID: 36102337) | RCT | 2,262 | 3 y | **No** cognitive benefit from cocoa extract | — |

**Consensus:** Short-term trials consistently show better endothelial function and small BP reductions. The definitive long-term trial (COSMOS) did not meet its primary cardiovascular endpoint, though a secondary reduction in cardiovascular death and supportive per-protocol analyses keep the door open. For cognition, the evidence is now clearly negative.

:::warning title="Where cocoa flavanols disappoint"
- **Cognition / memory** — COSMOS-Mind found no benefit; this is not a nootropic.
- **Primary cardiovascular prevention** — the largest trial missed its main endpoint; the case rests on a secondary signal.
- **Ordinary chocolate** — most of the flavanols are processed out; the trials used standardized extracts, not a candy bar.
:::

**What would change this grade.** The tier is **B** because the mechanism and surrogate outcomes are solid and one large trial showed a favorable CVD-death signal — but a *missed* primary endpoint caps it. It would rise with a trial meeting a primary cardiovascular endpoint, and fall if the CVD-death signal did not replicate.

## 5. Monitoring

Home blood pressure over 4–8 weeks. There is no useful "cognition" endpoint to track here — set expectations accordingly.

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Well tolerated.** Caffeine/theobromine content is low in flavanol extracts but non-zero — sensitive users should check the label.

:::redflag title="Do not self-start without clearance"
- **Caffeine/theobromine sensitivity or arrhythmia** — verify the extract's methylxanthine content.
:::

:::warning title="Who should skip cocoa flavanols"
- **People buying it for memory or dementia prevention** — the cognition data are negative.
- **Anyone expecting standard chocolate to substitute** — the flavanol dose isn't there.
:::
