> **TL;DR** — Cocoa flavanols are among the best-studied dietary compounds for the aging vascular system. They raise nitric-oxide availability, improve flow-mediated dilation, and lower blood pressure across dozens of RCTs — and in COSMOS, the largest trial (~21,000 adults), they cut cardiovascular deaths in the pre-specified secondary analysis. A genuinely strong, food-derived way to support endothelial health. 500–900 mg total flavanols/day, standardized.

## 1. What cocoa flavanols do (and why it matters)

The endothelium — the single-cell lining of every blood vessel — stiffens and loses nitric-oxide output with age, and that decline sits upstream of much of cardiovascular aging. Cocoa flavanols (chiefly epicatechin) are one of the most reliably vasoactive nutrients we have: a large Cochrane review of 40+ RCTs found consistent, if modest, blood-pressure lowering, and the 2022 COSMOS trial (n≈21,000) reported a **27% reduction in cardiovascular death** in its pre-specified secondary endpoint. That is real, large-scale human signal for a compound you can get from food.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** Altered intercellular communication · Chronic inflammation · Mitochondrial dysfunction

## 2. Mechanism

Epicatechin activates endothelial nitric-oxide synthase (eNOS), raising NO and relaxing vessels (better flow-mediated dilation). It also blunts NADPH-oxidase ROS and NF-κB-driven inflammation, and supports mitochondrial function in vascular tissue. The net effect is a more responsive, less inflamed vascular bed.

## 3. Evidence

COSMOS (Sesso et al., 2022) is the anchor — a large, long, placebo-controlled trial where the flavanol arm showed reduced cardiovascular mortality. Upstream of that, Flaviola and multiple meta-analyses show improved FMD and lower blood pressure. The honest framing: the vascular-function and BP effects are well established; the hard-outcome mortality signal is a strong secondary finding, not a primary win — which is why this sits at a confident Tier B.

## 4. Dosing

500–900 mg total cocoa flavanols per day, from a standardized extract (most chocolate is processed in ways that destroy flavanols, so a standardized source matters). Split doses are fine; take with food.

## 5. Monitoring

Track resting blood pressure over 8–12 weeks (home cuff, same time of day). If you test vascular function, flow-mediated dilation improves within hours to weeks. hs-CRP is a reasonable secondary marker.

## 6. Safety & red flags

Very well tolerated. Standardized flavanol extracts avoid the sugar, fat, and cadmium load of ordinary chocolate. Caffeine/theobromine content is low in flavanol extracts but check if you are sensitive. No major drug interactions at food-derived doses.

## 7. Synergies & stack integration

Pairs naturally with **dietary nitrate (beetroot)** and **omega-3** for a layered vascular-support stack (NO from two independent routes plus inflammation resolution). Complementary to a magnesium/potassium-adequate diet for blood pressure.
