> **TL;DR** — Aged garlic extract (AGE) is one of the best-evidenced natural cardiovascular supplements: multiple RCTs show it lowers systolic blood pressure by roughly 5–11 mmHg in people with uncontrolled hypertension, and imaging trials link it to slower coronary calcium progression. The 20-month aging step converts harsh allicin into stable, bioavailable S-allylcysteine — the benefit without the burn or the breath. 600–1,200 mg/day.

## 1. What aged garlic extract does (and why it matters)

Blood pressure and arterial plaque are two of the highest-leverage targets in cardiovascular aging, and AGE has genuine human data on both. Beyond lowering BP, imaging work links it to slower coronary calcium and soft-plaque progression — an outcome very few supplements can point to. The aging process (up to 20 months in controlled humidity) trades unstable allicin for stable, water-soluble S-allylcysteine (SAC), giving reproducible dosing and none of the GI harshness of raw garlic.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** Altered intercellular communication · Chronic inflammation · Deregulated nutrient sensing

## 2. Mechanism — hydrogen sulfide, nitric oxide, and a quieter artery wall

| Pathway | Mechanism | Hallmark link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H₂S / NO signaling | SAC raises hydrogen-sulfide and nitric-oxide tone → vasodilation, lower peripheral resistance | Altered intercellular communication |
| LDL oxidation | Inhibits LDL oxidation and foam-cell formation; lowers oxidized-phospholipid burden | Chronic inflammation |
| Platelet function | Mild antiplatelet effect → less thrombotic tendency | Altered intercellular communication |

The net effect is multi-pronged action on the drivers of atherosclerosis rather than a single lever.

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study | Design | N | Duration | Key outcome | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ried 2013 (PMID: 23169470) | RCT, dose-response | 79 | 12 wk | Systolic BP −11.8 mmHg vs placebo (2-capsule/day) | B |
| Ried 2010 (PMID: 20594781) | RCT | 50 | 12 wk | Systolic BP −10.2 mmHg in uncontrolled hypertension | B |
| Ahmadi/Budoff 2010 (PMID: 20152239) | RCT (AGE + supplement) | 60 | 12 mo | Less coronary-artery-calcium progression, better vascular function | B |
| Serrano 2023 (PMID: 37686723) | RCT, triple-blind | — | 12 wk | Smaller but significant BP drop with low-dose SAC black-garlic | B |

**Consensus:** For blood pressure in people already on treatment but not at goal, the effect is real and roughly on the order of a first-line lifestyle change. The plaque-progression signal is promising but rests on small trials, several using AGE combined with other supplements — so read it as supportive, not settled.

:::warning title="Where aged garlic disappoints"
- **Normotensive people** — the BP effect is concentrated in those with elevated or uncontrolled pressure; if yours is already normal, expect little movement.
- **"Detox" and immune claims** — the marketing outruns the data; the defensible story is cardiovascular.
- **Raw-garlic equivalence** — allicin-standardized and aged extracts are different products; SAC content, not "allicin potential," is what these trials used.
:::

**What would change this grade.** The tier is **B** because the BP evidence is consistent across independent RCTs but the hard-outcome (event) data are indirect. It would rise to **A** with a large trial showing reduced cardiovascular events, and fall if better-controlled imaging trials failed to reproduce the plaque signal.

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Very well tolerated** — the aged extract avoids most of the GI upset of raw garlic.
- **Mild antiplatelet activity** — the main practical caution.

:::redflag title="Do not self-start without clearance"
- **Anticoagulant / antiplatelet therapy** — additive bleeding risk; clear it with your prescriber.
- **Upcoming surgery** — stop 7–10 days beforehand per your surgeon.
:::

:::warning title="Who should skip aged garlic"
- **People on warfarin or DOACs** without physician oversight.
- **Anyone with a bleeding disorder.**
- **Those expecting an immune or "detox" benefit** — that's not what the evidence supports.
:::
