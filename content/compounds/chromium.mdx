> **TL;DR** — Chromium picolinate is marketed heavily for blood-sugar and weight management, but the human trial evidence is genuinely mixed and modest at best — some small trials show minor glycemic improvements in people with insulin resistance or type 2 diabetes, while larger and better-designed trials often show little to no effect versus placebo. Tier C; a low-priority, low-risk compound with a marketing reputation that outpaces its trial record.

## 1. What chromium does — and why the evidence is weaker than the marketing suggests

Chromium is a trace mineral proposed to enhance insulin signaling, and chromium picolinate specifically became one of the most heavily marketed "blood sugar support" and weight-loss supplements of the past few decades. The trial record, however, has not consistently supported that reputation: while some smaller studies — particularly in people with existing insulin resistance or type 2 diabetes — report modest improvements in fasting glucose or HbA1c, larger and more rigorous placebo-controlled trials in general or non-deficient populations frequently show no significant benefit. Whether chromium is even an essential nutrient with a defined human deficiency syndrome remains debated in the nutrition-science literature.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** [Disabled macroautophagy](/library/disabled-macroautophagy) (insulin signaling, proposed)

:::evidence tier="C"
Meta-analyses of chromium picolinate for glycemic control show a genuinely mixed picture: some pooled analyses find small, statistically significant reductions in fasting glucose or HbA1c, especially in diabetic populations, while others find no significant effect and note substantial heterogeneity and publication-bias concerns across the smaller trials. Weight-loss trial evidence is weaker still, generally showing no meaningful effect beyond placebo. Tier C: the evidence base does not support the strength of chromium's popular reputation.
:::

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Dose:** 200–1000 mcg/day chromium picolinate, matching the range used in most trials (with substantial heterogeneity).
- **Duration:** trials assessing glycemic markers typically ran 8–16 weeks.

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Generally well tolerated** at typical supplemental doses.
- **Kidney or liver disease** — case reports of toxicity exist at very high doses or with impaired clearance; physician oversight advised in these conditions.
- **On glucose-lowering medication** — theoretical additive effect; monitor blood sugar if combining.

:::redflag title="Coordinate before starting if any apply"
- **Kidney or liver disease** — physician oversight given rare toxicity reports
- **On insulin or other glucose-lowering medication** — monitor for additive effect
:::
