> **TL;DR** — Astaxanthin is a marine carotenoid antioxidant whose molecular structure lets it sit across the entire cell membrane and quench free radicals at both the hydrophilic and hydrophobic ends simultaneously — a genuine structural advantage over vitamin C or E alone. Human RCTs show benefits for skin photoaging, eye/visual fatigue, and exercise-oxidative-stress markers at 4–12 mg/day — well above the ~2 mg found in most krill oil capsules. Tier B; a real but adjunct antioxidant, not a foundation compound.

## 1. What astaxanthin does

Astaxanthin is a red-orange carotenoid produced by the microalgae *Haematococcus pluvialis* (and concentrated up the food chain in salmon and krill) that gives those organisms their color. Structurally, it's unusual among antioxidants: its long polyene chain spans the full width of a cell membrane, with polar end-groups at both membrane surfaces — letting it neutralize reactive oxygen species at both the membrane interior and its aqueous interfaces, a broader coverage than single-compartment antioxidants like vitamin C (aqueous) or vitamin E (lipid) alone.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** [Chronic inflammation](/library/chronic-inflammation) · [Mitochondrial dysfunction](/library/mitochondrial-dysfunction) · [Loss of proteostasis](/library/loss-of-proteostasis) (skin/UV protection)

:::evidence tier="B"
Human RCTs at 4–12 mg/day report improved skin elasticity and reduced UV-induced photoaging markers (Tominaga et al., *Acta Biochim Pol*, 2012), reduced eye/visual fatigue in screen-heavy work (Nagaki et al., *J Trad Med*, 2002), and reduced exercise-induced oxidative stress and improved endurance markers in several smaller trials. Effect sizes are modest and study populations are often small. Tier B: real, replicated human data at meaningful doses, not foundational-tier evidence.
:::

:::practical title="Dose matters — most krill oil doesn't count"
The therapeutic doses in positive human trials are **4–12 mg/day**. Krill oil products typically contain only ~1–2 mg astaxanthin per serving (present mainly as a stabilizing antioxidant for the oil, not a therapeutic dose) — don't assume krill oil is delivering an active astaxanthin dose.
:::

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Dose:** 4–12 mg/day, matching the positive trial range.
- **Form:** natural (*Haematococcus pluvialis*-derived) astaxanthin, taken with a fat-containing meal (fat-soluble carotenoid).
- **Duration:** 8–12 weeks to judge skin/eye endpoints.

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Well tolerated** at 4–12 mg/day in trials; skin may take on a mild orange tint at very high sustained doses (carotenoderma), which is harmless and reversible.
- **Antiplatelet effect** — mild, at higher doses; combine cautiously with other blood-thinning supplements or medications.
- **Fat-soluble** — take with dietary fat for absorption.

:::redflag title="Coordinate before starting if any apply"
- **On anticoagulant/antiplatelet medication** — mild additive effect possible
- **Pregnancy or breastfeeding** — insufficient supplemental-dose safety data
:::
