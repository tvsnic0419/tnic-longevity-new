> **TL;DR** — Iodine is required to synthesize thyroid hormone, and both deficiency and excess cause thyroid dysfunction — this is a genuine "Goldilocks" nutrient where more is not better. Correcting real deficiency is well-established, essential medicine; high-dose iodine supplementation in replete individuals (common in some "detox" and kelp-based products) can trigger thyroid dysfunction, including in people with no prior thyroid history. Tier B; test before megadosing.

## 1. What iodine does

Iodine is a required structural component of thyroid hormones (T4 and T3), which regulate metabolic rate, growth, and development across nearly every tissue. Iodine deficiency is a well-recognized global public-health issue (the reason for mandatory iodization of salt in many countries) causing goiter, hypothyroidism, and — critically during pregnancy — impaired fetal neurodevelopment. What's less widely known: **excess** iodine can also cause thyroid dysfunction (both hypo- and hyperthyroidism), particularly in people with pre-existing thyroid autoimmunity, making this a nutrient where the "more is better" instinct is specifically wrong.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** [Altered intercellular communication](/library/altered-intercellular-communication) (thyroid hormone synthesis)

:::evidence tier="B"
Iodine deficiency correction is well-established, essential public-health medicine — one of the clearest deficiency-correction stories in nutrition science. The excess-iodine thyroid-dysfunction literature (including case reports and cohort data related to high-dose kelp/seaweed supplements) is also well documented, particularly in people with underlying thyroid autoimmunity (Hashimoto's) who can be triggered into dysfunction by iodine loads that would be harmless in someone without that susceptibility. Tier B: strong evidence in both directions — deficiency correction and excess-avoidance both matter.
:::

:::practical title="Test before adding a high-dose iodine or kelp supplement"
Most people in countries with iodized salt get adequate iodine from diet. High-dose iodine or kelp supplements (common in some "thyroid support" and "detox" products) are more likely to cause harm than benefit in someone without a documented deficiency — especially if undiagnosed thyroid autoimmunity is present.
:::

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Dietary sufficiency:** iodized salt, dairy, and seafood typically provide adequate intake in most developed-country diets.
- **If a deficiency is confirmed:** correct under physician guidance, typically via standard multivitamin-level iodine (150 mcg/day range) rather than high-dose kelp products.
- **Avoid:** high-dose kelp or "thyroid support" iodine supplements without a confirmed deficiency and physician guidance.

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Both deficiency and excess cause thyroid dysfunction** — this is the central safety framing for iodine, unlike most nutrients where only deficiency is a concern.
- **Thyroid autoimmunity (Hashimoto's, Graves')** — a real, documented trigger risk from high-dose iodine exposure.
- **Amiodarone and iodine-containing medications** — can compound iodine load; coordinate with a physician if on such medications.

:::redflag title="Coordinate before high-dose supplementation if any apply"
- **Known thyroid autoimmunity** — avoid high-dose iodine without endocrinologist guidance
- **On amiodarone or other iodine-containing medications** — coordinate to avoid excess total iodine load
- **Any new thyroid symptoms after starting a high-dose iodine product** — stop and get TSH tested
:::
