> **TL;DR** — Alpha-GPC is a highly bioavailable choline source with human RCT data for cognitive function in aging/post-stroke populations and acute growth-hormone/power-output effects in athletes. The honest caveat: a 2021 observational study linked long-term high-choline intake (via TMAO, a gut-microbiome metabolite) to elevated stroke risk — a real, debated signal worth knowing, not a reason to conclude choline is unsafe at moderate doses. Tier B; 300–600 mg/day.

## 1. What Alpha-GPC does

Alpha-GPC (L-alpha-glycerylphosphorylcholine) is a choline compound that crosses the blood-brain barrier more readily than most choline sources, making it a precursor for **acetylcholine**, the neurotransmitter central to memory and neuromuscular signaling. It has two genuinely separate evidence threads: cognitive support in aging and post-stroke populations (where it's an approved prescription drug in some countries), and acute athletic performance — modest growth-hormone and power-output effects in short-term trials.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** [Altered intercellular communication](/library/altered-intercellular-communication) (cholinergic signaling)

:::evidence tier="B"
Alpha-GPC is an approved prescription drug for cognitive decline in some countries (notably Italy), backed by RCTs in vascular cognitive impairment and post-stroke populations showing improved cognitive measures. Separately, smaller trials in healthy athletes show acute increases in growth hormone and modest improvements in power output after single doses. A 2021 observational study (Zhu et al. and related TMAO literature) associated higher circulating **TMAO** — a gut-bacteria metabolite of dietary choline (and of choline-rich foods generally, not Alpha-GPC specifically) — with increased stroke risk in some cohorts; this is a genuine, actively debated signal in the choline literature, not settled science, and it applies to choline intake broadly (eggs, meat, and supplements alike), not uniquely to Alpha-GPC. Tier B: real cognitive and performance evidence, with an honest, unresolved safety question in the background literature worth knowing about.
:::

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Cognitive support:** 300–600 mg/day, matching most trial doses.
- **Acute performance use:** 300–600 mg, 30–60 minutes pre-exercise, based on the athlete trials.
- **Avoid chronic very-high-dose stacking** with other choline sources (e.g., large amounts of CDP-choline or dietary choline supplements simultaneously) without a specific reason.

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Well tolerated** at 300–600 mg/day; headache and GI upset are the main reported complaints (often from excess acetylcholine activity).
- **TMAO / cardiovascular literature** — an active area of research affecting choline broadly; those with significant cardiovascular disease history should discuss with a physician before chronic high-dose use.
- **Cholinergic side effects** — sweating, salivation at high doses.

:::redflag title="Coordinate before starting if any apply"
- **History of stroke or significant cardiovascular disease** — discuss the choline/TMAO literature with your physician
- **On cholinesterase-affecting medications** — possible interaction; coordinate with your prescriber
:::
