> **TL;DR** — L-theanine is the amino acid in green tea that produces "relaxed alertness" — it raises alpha brain-wave activity and takes the edge off caffeine's jitter. Its best-documented cognitive benefit is *in combination with caffeine* (sharper attention, less distractibility), and much of that data is industry-funded. As a calm-focus and sleep-quality aid it's low-risk and useful. 100–200 mg.

## 1. What L-theanine does (and why it matters)

Stress reactivity and poor sleep quietly accelerate cognitive aging. L-theanine shifts the brain toward a calm-but-alert state (more alpha activity), blunts the sympathetic "wired" feeling from caffeine, and modestly supports sleep quality and subjective relaxation. It's a lifestyle-buffer more than a disease-modifier — but a clean, well-tolerated one.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** Altered intercellular communication

## 2. Mechanism — alpha waves and glutamate modulation

L-theanine crosses the blood-brain barrier and increases alpha-band EEG activity associated with relaxed attention. It modulates glutamatergic signaling (a structural analog of glutamate) and nudges GABA, dopamine, and serotonin — the combination that produces calm focus without sedation.

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study | Design | N | Duration | Key outcome | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Owen 2008 (PMID: 18681988) | RCT, crossover | 27 | Acute | L-theanine + caffeine improved attention-switching, cut distractibility | B |
| Giesbrecht 2010 (PMID: 21040626) | RCT, double-blind | 44 | Acute | 97 mg theanine + 40 mg caffeine improved accuracy and alertness | B |

**Consensus:** The reproducible cognitive finding is that theanine plus caffeine sharpens attention and reduces the jittery downside of caffeine alone. Theanine on its own shows relaxation and modest anxiety/sleep-quality benefits, but the attention data are strongest in the combination — and several of those trials come from a tea-industry lab.

:::warning title="Where L-theanine disappoints"
- **Standalone nootropic claims** — most of the crisp cognitive data are the caffeine *combination*, not theanine by itself.
- **Clinical anxiety/insomnia** — effects are mild; it is not a treatment for a diagnosed disorder.
- **Longevity outcomes** — there are none; this is a stress/sleep/attention buffer, graded on surrogates.
:::

**What would change this grade.** The tier is **B** because acute cognitive and relaxation effects replicate, but they're mostly caffeine-paired and short-term. It would rise with independent, longer-term trials of theanine alone on stress or sleep outcomes, and fall if those showed no standalone benefit.

## 4. Dosing protocol

| Parameter | Recommendation | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dose | 100–200 mg | Often paired ~2:1 with caffeine for focus |
| Timing | With caffeine (focus) or evening (sleep) | Non-sedating by day |
| Duration | Works acutely | No loading needed |

## 5. Monitoring

Track subjective calm, focus, and sleep quality. If pairing with caffeine, note whether the usual jitter/crash is reduced. No labs required.

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Very well tolerated** — one of the safest nootropics; it's a tea constituent.
- **Mild blood-pressure lowering** — usually trivial.

:::redflag title="Do not self-start without clearance"
- **Blood-pressure medication** — theoretical additive BP lowering; monitor if you already run low.
:::

:::warning title="Who should skip L-theanine"
- **People expecting a strong standalone cognitive boost** — pair it with caffeine or reset expectations.
- **Anyone on layered blood-pressure-lowering agents** who runs hypotensive.
:::

## 7. Synergies and antagonists

**Pairs well with:**

| Partner | Rationale | Guide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Caffeine | The classic calm-focus pairing — the best-evidenced use | Focus stack |
| Citicoline | Adds cholinergic/membrane support to attention | Cognition stack |
| Lion's mane | Longer-horizon plasticity alongside acute calm focus | Cognition stack |

**Works against / redundant with:** No well-characterized antagonists at typical doses. Layering multiple sedating agents (theanine + magnesium + glycine at night) is usually complementary, not conflicting — but start one at a time.
