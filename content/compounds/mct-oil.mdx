> **TL;DR** — MCT oil is rapidly absorbed and largely bypasses normal fat digestion, going straight to the liver where it's converted to ketones — a genuinely fast, reliable way to raise ketone levels without a strict ketogenic diet. Human evidence supports acute ketone elevation and some appetite/metabolic effects; long-term cognitive or longevity-outcome claims are much thinner. Tier B; 1–2 tbsp/day, titrated up to avoid GI upset.

## 1. What MCT oil does

Medium-chain triglycerides (typically C8/caprylic and C10/capric acid, sometimes with C6/C12) are absorbed directly into the portal bloodstream and transported to the liver without needing bile-salt emulsification the way long-chain fats do — and once there, they're preferentially oxidized and converted into **ketone bodies**, even without carbohydrate restriction. This makes MCT oil the most reliable non-fasting way to acutely raise blood ketone levels, which is why it's used clinically (as a component of ketogenic diet therapy for epilepsy) and popularly (for "keto without strict keto").

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** [Mitochondrial dysfunction](/library/mitochondrial-dysfunction) (alternative fuel source) · [Altered intercellular communication](/library/altered-intercellular-communication) (cognitive fuel)

:::evidence tier="B"
Human pharmacokinetic studies consistently show MCT oil reliably raises blood ketone levels within hours, independent of carbohydrate intake — well-established, reproducible physiology. Some trials show modest appetite-suppression and metabolic-rate effects versus equivalent long-chain fat. Cognitive-benefit trials (particularly in mild cognitive impairment, exploiting the brain's ability to use ketones as fuel when glucose metabolism is impaired) show promising but smaller, shorter-duration signals. Tier B: solid physiological/ketone-elevation evidence, thinner on long-term cognitive or longevity outcomes.
:::

:::practical title="Titrate slowly — GI tolerance is the real limiting factor"
MCT oil's most common real-world problem isn't efficacy, it's **tolerability**: starting at a full dose commonly causes GI cramping and diarrhea. Start with 1 teaspoon and titrate up over 1–2 weeks to 1–2 tablespoons/day.
:::

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Start:** 1 teaspoon (5 mL)/day with food, titrating up over 1–2 weeks.
- **Target:** 1–2 tablespoons (15–30 mL)/day, split across meals.
- **Form:** C8-predominant ("caprylic acid") products convert to ketones fastest; C8/C10 blends are more affordable and still effective.

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **GI upset** (cramping, diarrhea) is the most common issue, dose-dependent and usually resolved with slower titration.
- **Liver disease** — MCTs are processed directly and rapidly by the liver; physician oversight advised with significant hepatic impairment.
- **Caloric density** — MCT oil is calorie-dense (~8.3 kcal/g); account for it in total intake if managing weight.

:::redflag title="Coordinate before starting if any apply"
- **Cirrhosis or significant liver disease** — physician oversight, given direct hepatic processing
- **Pancreatitis or fat-malabsorption conditions** — discuss with a physician, as MCTs are sometimes used therapeutically in these conditions and dosing should be individualized
:::
