> **TL;DR** — Nattokinase is an enzyme derived from natto (fermented soybeans) with real fibrinolytic (clot-dissolving) activity demonstrated in lab and small human studies, plus some human RCT data for modest blood-pressure reduction. The evidence base for cardiovascular-event prevention is preliminary — small trials, short duration — and the fibrinolytic mechanism itself is the reason this compound needs the most careful safety framing in this library. Tier C; genuinely interesting mechanism, real bleeding-risk interaction profile, physician coordination essential if combining with any blood-thinning therapy.

## 1. What nattokinase does

Nattokinase is a serine protease enzyme extracted from natto, a traditional Japanese fermented soybean food. In vitro and small human studies, it demonstrates **fibrinolytic activity** — it can help break down fibrin, the protein mesh that forms blood clots — which is the basis for interest in it as a cardiovascular-support compound. Small human trials also report modest blood-pressure reductions. This is a genuinely interesting mechanism with a real biological effect; it's also, precisely because of that mechanism, one of the compounds in this library where interaction risk deserves the most careful attention.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** [Chronic inflammation](/library/chronic-inflammation) (cardiovascular support)

:::evidence tier="C"
Nattokinase's fibrinolytic activity is demonstrated in vitro and in small human pharmacodynamic studies (showing measurable effects on clotting-related lab markers after dosing). Small RCTs report modest blood-pressure reductions. However, the evidence base for hard cardiovascular-event outcomes (heart attack, stroke prevention) is preliminary — small sample sizes, short durations, and limited independent replication at the scale of this library's Tier A compounds. Tier C: a real, measurable biological effect with a genuine but early-stage outcome-evidence base, and a safety profile that requires more caution than most Tier C compounds.
:::

:::practical title="The mechanism that makes it interesting is the mechanism that makes it risky"
Nattokinase's clot-dissolving activity is exactly why it needs careful handling: combined with anticoagulant or antiplatelet medication (or even other supplements with mild antiplatelet activity, like high-dose fish oil or vitamin E), the bleeding-risk interaction is real, not theoretical. This is not a compound to add casually to an existing cardiovascular medication regimen without physician awareness.
:::

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Dose:** 100 mg (approximately 2000 FU, fibrinolytic units)/day is a commonly studied range.
- **Timing:** away from meals for some formulations (check product-specific guidance), as food may affect enzyme activity.
- **Physician awareness required** if you take any blood-thinning medication, are scheduled for surgery, or have a bleeding disorder.

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Bleeding risk is the central safety concern** — nattokinase's fibrinolytic mechanism means it can meaningfully increase bleeding risk, especially combined with other blood-thinning agents.
- **Surgery/dental procedures** — discontinue well in advance per your surgeon's guidance, given the clot-dissolving mechanism.
- **Soy allergy** — nattokinase is soy-derived; avoid if allergic.
- **Pregnancy** — avoid; insufficient safety data and a mechanism (clotting effects) that is specifically relevant to pregnancy risk.

:::redflag title="This compound requires more caution than most Tier C entries"
- **On any anticoagulant or antiplatelet medication (including daily aspirin)** — do not combine without explicit physician approval
- **Upcoming surgery or dental work** — discontinue per your surgeon's specific guidance
- **Bleeding disorder or recent hemorrhagic event** — avoid entirely
- **Soy allergy** — avoid (soy-derived enzyme)
- **Pregnancy** — avoid
:::
