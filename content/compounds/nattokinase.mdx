> **TL;DR** — Nattokinase is a fibrinolytic enzyme from fermented soybean (natto). A meta-analysis of RCTs shows it modestly lowers systolic and diastolic blood pressure, and trials show favorable effects on fibrinolysis markers. Genuinely useful for cardiovascular support — but it thins the blood, so it's the one on this list with a real interaction to respect. 100 mg (~2,000 FU)/day.

## 1. What nattokinase does (and why it matters)

Aging blood gets more thrombotic and stiffer, and blood pressure creeps up. Nattokinase acts on both fronts: it directly degrades fibrin (the protein scaffold of clots), enhances the body's own clot-dissolving system, and lowers blood pressure in controlled trials. That combination — mild antihypertensive plus profibrinolytic — is unusual for a supplement.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** Altered intercellular communication · Chronic inflammation

## 2. Mechanism — direct fibrinolysis plus blood-pressure lowering

Nattokinase is a serine protease (subtilisin NAT) that cleaves fibrin directly and boosts endogenous fibrinolysis (raising plasmin activity and reducing markers like von Willebrand factor). Blood-pressure lowering appears to involve reduced angiotensin-converting-enzyme activity and improved vascular tone. The net effect is less thrombotic tendency and modestly lower pressure.

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study | Design | N | Duration | Key outcome | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Li 2023 (PMID: 39076715) | Meta-analysis (6 RCTs) | 546 | — | SBP −3.45 / DBP −2.32 mmHg; lipid effects mixed; slight glucose rise | B |
| Jensen 2016 (PMID: 27785095) | RCT, double-blind | 79 | 8 wk | 100 mg/d lowered BP and von Willebrand factor (N. American cohort) | B |

**Consensus:** Across trials nattokinase reliably nudges blood pressure down and improves fibrinolytic markers, and it now has data outside the original Asian cohorts. The lipid effects are inconsistent (dose-dependent and sometimes unfavorable), and there are no hard-outcome trials — so the defensible story is BP plus fibrinolysis, not cholesterol.

:::warning title="Where nattokinase disappoints"
- **Lipids** — the meta-analysis found mixed, dose-dependent effects; don't take it for cholesterol.
- **Hard outcomes** — no stroke/heart-attack prevention trials exist; the case rests on surrogates.
- **"More is better"** — higher total doses looked *worse* for some lipid markers; this isn't a dose-to-effect lever.
:::

**What would change this grade.** The tier is **B** because a clean meta-analysis supports the BP and fibrinolysis effects, but there are no clinical-outcome trials. It would rise with event data; it would fall if larger trials failed to reproduce the BP effect.

| Form | Vitamin-K2-reduced (e.g. NSK-SD) if on warfarin considerations | Natto is naturally K2-rich |

## 5. Monitoring

Home blood pressure over 4–8 weeks. If you are on any antithrombotic therapy, your clinician may want to watch for bruising/bleeding rather than a specific lab.

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **The key caution on this list** — nattokinase is fibrinolytic; it meaningfully affects clotting.

:::redflag title="Do not self-start without clearance"
- **Anticoagulants / antiplatelets (warfarin, DOACs, aspirin, clopidogrel)** — additive bleeding risk; requires physician oversight.
- **Bleeding disorders or upcoming surgery** — stop well in advance; clear with your surgeon.
- **Recent stroke or active bleeding** — avoid.
:::

:::warning title="Who should skip nattokinase"
- **Anyone on blood thinners** without explicit medical supervision.
- **People with bleeding disorders.**
- **Those taking it for cholesterol** — the lipid data don't support that use.
:::

**Works against / redundant with:** The important interaction is *additive*, not antagonistic — stacking nattokinase with other blood-thinning agents (fish oil, high-dose garlic, aspirin) compounds bleeding risk. Coordinate the whole antithrombotic load with a clinician rather than adding piecemeal.
