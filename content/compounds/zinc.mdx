> **TL;DR** — Zinc is an essential trace mineral for immune function, wound healing, and hundreds of enzymes, and mild deficiency is common in older adults. Human trials (including Cochrane-reviewed data) show zinc lozenges can shorten common-cold duration when started early. The critical caveat: chronic **excess** zinc causes copper deficiency and can itself impair immunity — this is a "correct a real deficit" compound, not a "more is better" one. Tier B; 15–30 mg/day, balanced with copper.

## 1. What zinc does

Zinc is a cofactor for hundreds of enzymes and a structural component of many transcription factors ("zinc fingers"), with outsized roles in immune-cell function, wound healing, and taste/smell. Mild zinc deficiency becomes more common with age due to reduced dietary intake and absorption, and it plausibly contributes to the age-related decline in immune function ("immunosenescence"). Zinc's most robust human evidence, though, is for something specific and well-studied: **shortening the duration of the common cold** when zinc lozenges are started within 24 hours of symptom onset.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** [Chronic inflammation](/library/chronic-inflammation) (immune function) · [Loss of proteostasis](/library/loss-of-proteostasis) (wound healing, enzyme cofactor)

:::evidence tier="B"
A Cochrane systematic review (Singh & Das) found zinc lozenges, started within 24 hours of cold symptoms, reduced the duration of common-cold symptoms by about 1–2 days versus placebo — genuine, replicated human RCT evidence for a specific, narrow indication. Broader immune-aging and deficiency-correction data is observational and mechanistic (Tier B), not from large longevity-outcome RCTs. Excess zinc's copper-deficiency and immune-impairment risk is well documented clinically. Tier B overall: real for correcting deficiency and cold-duration reduction; not established as a broad longevity lever at high doses.
:::

:::practical title="This is a correction compound, not a megadose compound"
Zinc's value is almost entirely in **correcting a real or likely deficit** (common in older adults, vegetarians/vegans, and those with GI conditions) and in the specific cold-duration application. High-dose, long-term zinc without a deficiency is more likely to cause copper deficiency and immune harm than additional benefit.
:::

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study | Design | Key outcomes | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cochrane review (Singh & Das) | Meta-analysis of human RCTs | Zinc lozenges (started ≤24h) reduced cold duration ~1–2 days | A (for this specific indication) |
| Zinc deficiency / immunosenescence literature | Observational + mechanistic | Plausible contributor to age-related immune decline | B |

**Consensus:** Tier B overall — a genuinely evidence-backed compound for its specific applications (deficiency correction, cold-duration reduction), not a broad high-dose longevity lever.

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Maintenance:** 15–30 mg/day elemental zinc, ideally paired with 1–2 mg copper to maintain balance.
- **Cold treatment:** zinc acetate or gluconate lozenges (matching Cochrane-reviewed doses), started within 24 hours of symptom onset, continued through the illness.
- **Take with food** to reduce GI upset; avoid taking simultaneously with high-dose calcium/iron, which compete for absorption.

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Well tolerated** at 15–30 mg/day; nausea is the most common short-term complaint (take with food).
- **Chronic excess zinc causes copper deficiency** — a real, well-documented clinical syndrome (anemia, neuropathy, impaired immunity) from sustained high-dose zinc without copper.
- **Nasal zinc products** — intranasal zinc has been linked to permanent loss of smell (anosmia); avoid nasal-spray zinc products entirely.
- **Drug interactions** — can reduce absorption of certain antibiotics (tetracyclines, fluoroquinolones); space doses.

:::redflag title="Coordinate before starting if any apply"
- **Long-term high-dose zinc without copper** — real copper-deficiency risk
- **Never use intranasal zinc products** — associated with permanent anosmia
- **On tetracycline/fluoroquinolone antibiotics** — space dosing by several hours
:::
