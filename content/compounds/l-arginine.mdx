> **TL;DR** — L-Arginine is the substrate for endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS) — the enzyme that produces nitric oxide (NO) to relax blood vessels. Real RCT evidence for mild blood pressure reduction, endothelial dysfunction improvement, and erectile dysfunction. **Post-MI use is contraindicated** — the VINTAGE-MI trial (Schulman 2006 PMID 16391215) showed increased mortality in myocardial infarction survivors given arginine. Standard dose 3–6 g/day, but citrulline is often a better choice (higher plasma arginine per dose, better GI tolerance).

## 1. What L-arginine does (and why it matters)

L-Arginine is a conditionally-essential amino acid — the sole substrate for **eNOS** (endothelial NO synthase), which produces the ubiquitous vasodilator nitric oxide. Under normal conditions, dietary arginine plus endogenous synthesis from citrulline is sufficient; under stress (surgery, sepsis, immune activation), arginine becomes essential.

The rationale for supplementation is that:
- **Endothelial dysfunction** (impaired NO production) is a hallmark of aging vascular biology
- **Aging arginase activity rises** — arginase competes with eNOS for the same substrate, diverting arginine to urea rather than NO
- **Cardiovascular risk factors** (hypertension, hypercholesterolemia, diabetes) all reduce NO availability

But the story has a twist: **arginine at high dose can worsen outcomes in advanced cardiovascular disease.** VINTAGE-MI showed post-MI supplementation increased mortality, likely because uncoupled eNOS in damaged tissue produces peroxynitrite rather than NO.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** Altered intercellular communication (endothelial NO) · Chronic inflammation (endothelial function)

:::evidence tier="B"
Chen et al. (1999, PMID 10404492) — chronic oral arginine improved erectile function in mild-moderate ED. Bode-Böger et al. (2003) — arginine reversed endothelial dysfunction in dyslipidemia. Menon et al. (2004) — post-MI infusion improved endothelial function. **VINTAGE-MI (Schulman 2006, PMID 16391215)** — 153 post-MI patients randomized to L-arginine 3 g TID × 6 months — trial stopped early due to 6 deaths in arginine arm vs 0 in placebo. Dong et al. (2011) meta — modest blood pressure reduction (~5 mmHg SBP). Citrulline supplementation (Ochiai 2012) may be preferable — better raises plasma arginine than arginine itself.
:::

## 2. Mechanism

| Pathway | Mechanism | Hallmark link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| eNOS substrate | L-Arginine → NO + L-citrulline (via eNOS) | Communication |
| Endothelium-dependent vasodilation | NO → cGMP → smooth muscle relaxation | Communication |
| Growth hormone (mild) | Arginine acutely stimulates pituitary GH release | Communication |
| Urea cycle | Substrate for hepatic urea cycle | — |
| Arginase substrate | Competing pathway; consumes arginine, produces polyamines | Cell signaling |

The **VINTAGE-MI signal** is the critical caveat: arginine in the setting of damaged, dysfunctional endothelium (uncoupled eNOS) can produce peroxynitrite instead of NO, worsening tissue injury. This is why arginine is not a routine post-MI supplement.

:::practical title="When arginine (or citrulline) is worth using"
Arginine is worth thought when **one or more** apply:

- Mild-moderate erectile dysfunction (proven RCT indication)
- Endothelial dysfunction with normal LV function (adjunct)
- Modest hypertension not requiring pharmacotherapy
- Building an NO-supporting stack (usually citrulline is preferred over arginine)

**Skip or defer** if you're post-MI, have severe heart failure, active herpes flare (arginine can worsen), or expect large effects on athletic performance (evidence is mixed).
:::

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study | Design | N | Duration | Key outcomes | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chen 1999 (PMID 10404492) | RCT (ED) | 50 | 6 wk | ↑ Erectile function | B |
| Bode-Böger 2003 | RCT (endothelium) | Small | Weeks | ↑ FMD in dyslipidemia | B |
| Dong 2011 meta | Meta (BP) | 11 RCTs | Various | ↓ SBP ~5 mmHg | B |
| **VINTAGE-MI 2006 (PMID 16391215)** | RCT (post-MI) | 153 | Stopped early | **↑ Mortality in arginine arm** | A (harm) |
| Ochiai 2012 (PMID 22732208) | Crossover (arginine vs citrulline) | 8 | Days | Citrulline raised plasma arg more than arg itself | B |

**Consensus:** Tier B for erectile dysfunction and mild endothelial support. Tier A NEGATIVE for post-MI use (VINTAGE-MI). Citrulline is often the smarter alternative — see `l-citrulline.mdx`.

:::warning title="Where arginine disappoints"
- **VINTAGE-MI** — post-MI arginine kills people; do not use in that context.
- **GI tolerance** — high oral doses (>6 g) cause nausea, diarrhea; citrulline is much better tolerated.
- **First-pass metabolism** — much of oral arginine is metabolized by intestinal arginase; plasma arginine rise is modest.
- **Herpes reactivation** — high-dose arginine + low lysine can reactivate HSV in susceptible individuals.
:::

## 4. Dosing protocol

| Parameter | Recommendation | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Erectile dysfunction | 3–5 g/day divided BID | Chen protocol; consider citrulline instead |
| Blood pressure / endothelial | 3–6 g/day divided | Citrulline often preferred |
| Pre-workout (mild) | 3 g pre-exercise | Very modest effect; citrulline malate more studied |
| Timing | Empty stomach or between meals | Improves absorption |
| Duration | 4–8 weeks for endothelial assessment | — |

## 5. Monitoring

| Biomarker | Target | Frequency | Action if off-target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Blood pressure | Trending down | Weekly | Combined with lifestyle |
| Erectile function (IIEF if that's goal) | Improving | 4 wk | If no effect, consider citrulline |
| GI tolerance | Normal | Ongoing | Reduce dose or switch to citrulline |

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **GI upset** at doses >6 g common.
- **VINTAGE-MI concern** — do not use post-MI.
- **HSV reactivation** in susceptible individuals.

:::redflag title="Do not self-start without clearance"
- **Recent myocardial infarction** — VINTAGE-MI contraindication.
- **Severe heart failure (EF <35%)** — vasodilation may worsen; MD-managed.
- **Active HSV flare** — arginine can promote viral replication.
- **On sildenafil / PDE5 inhibitors** — additive vasodilation; usually OK but monitor.
- **On nitrates for angina** — additive hypotension.
- **Renal or hepatic failure** — amino acid load caution.
:::
