> **TL;DR** — Boron is a trace mineral with a real, if under-studied, role in steroid hormone metabolism, vitamin D activation, and bone/joint health. Small human trials suggest modest effects on free testosterone and joint comfort, but the evidence base is thin — far smaller and less rigorous than the Tier A/B compounds in this library. Tier C: plausible and low-risk, not a priority addition. 3–6 mg/day.

## 1. What boron does

Boron is a trace mineral found in fruits, vegetables, nuts, and legumes that most diets supply in small amounts. It has a genuine, if incompletely characterized, biochemical role: it appears to influence steroid hormone metabolism (small trials report modest increases in free testosterone and reductions in estradiol in some populations), support vitamin D activation and metabolism, and play a role in bone and cartilage mineralization — plausible enough that it circulates in both bone-health and hormone-support supplement categories.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** [Loss of proteostasis](/library/loss-of-proteostasis) (bone/joint) · [Altered intercellular communication](/library/altered-intercellular-communication) (steroid hormone metabolism)

:::evidence tier="C"
Small human trials (typically n<50, short duration) report modest effects on free testosterone, estradiol, and inflammatory markers, and some suggest joint-comfort benefits in osteoarthritis. These are genuinely interesting signals, but the evidence base is thin relative to compounds elsewhere in this library — small sample sizes, short durations, and limited independent replication. Tier C: plausible mechanism with early human data, not yet a well-established intervention.
:::

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Dose:** 3–6 mg/day, matching the range used in most small trials.
- **Form:** boron citrate, glycinate, or calcium fructoborate are common forms; no strong evidence one form is clearly superior.
- **Duration:** trial effects, where reported, were assessed over 4–12 weeks.

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Well tolerated** at typical supplemental doses (3–6 mg/day).
- **High chronic doses** (well above supplemental range) have shown reproductive-toxicity signals in animal studies — a reason to stay within established supplemental ranges, not a reason for alarm at normal doses.
- **Limited long-term human safety data** at any dose, given the smaller evidence base overall.

:::redflag title="Stay within typical supplemental doses"
- **Do not exceed established supplemental ranges** (3–6 mg/day) given limited long-term human data
- **Pregnancy or breastfeeding** — avoid supplemental doses above dietary intake given the animal reproductive-toxicity signal at high doses
:::
