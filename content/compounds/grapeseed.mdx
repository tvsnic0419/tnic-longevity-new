> **TL;DR** — Grape seed extract standardized to ≥95% OPC (oligomeric proanthocyanidins) is a multi-pathway vascular antioxidant — 20–50× stronger than vitamin C in ROS assays. Tier B: a 16-RCT meta-analysis showed meaningful blood-pressure reduction. 150–300 mg standardized OPC/day; retest blood pressure and hs-CRP at week 12.

## 1. What grape seed extract does (and why it matters)

Grape seed extract's active fraction is a family of proanthocyanidins (OPCs) that act on four fronts at once: they scavenge reactive oxygen species, activate the NRF2 antioxidant program, upregulate endothelial nitric oxide (eNOS/NO), and suppress NF-κB inflammatory signaling. The standardization number matters enormously — only extracts standardized to ≥95% OPC deliver the concentrations used in trials. Its clearest human signal is vascular: improved blood pressure and endothelial function.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** Chronic inflammation · Genomic instability · Mitochondrial dysfunction · Altered intercellular communication

:::evidence tier="B"
PMID 27082595 — Zhang et al., *Medicine* (2016). Meta-analysis of 16 randomized controlled trials found grape seed extract significantly reduced systolic blood pressure, with the largest effect in younger and metabolically at-risk subgroups.
:::

## 2. Mechanism — one extract, four antioxidant levers

Unlike single-target antioxidants, OPCs engage overlapping defensive pathways. Direct ROS scavenging handles the immediate oxidative load; NRF2 activation transcribes the cell's own antioxidant enzymes for a durable effect; eNOS upregulation improves endothelial vasodilation; and NF-κB suppression lowers the inflammatory tone that drives vascular aging.

| Pathway | Mechanism | Hallmark link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ROS scavenging | Direct neutralization of reactive oxygen species | Genomic instability |
| NRF2 activation | Transcribes endogenous antioxidant enzymes | Mitochondrial dysfunction |
| eNOS / NO | Improves endothelial vasodilation and blood pressure | Altered intercellular communication |
| NF-κB suppression | Lowers inflammatory signaling tone | Chronic inflammation |

:::practical title="When grape seed extract is worth your money"
Grape seed extract earns a stack slot when **two or more** apply:

- Borderline-elevated blood pressure or reduced endothelial function
- Elevated oxidative-stress or inflammatory markers
- You want a broad vascular antioxidant alongside a targeted NRF2 agent

**Skip or defer** if you take anticoagulants without oversight (OPCs have mild antiplatelet activity) or if your antioxidant coverage (sulforaphane, R-ALA) is already strong.
:::

**Consensus:** Tier B — the blood-pressure meta-analysis is solid and the mechanism is well characterized, but grape seed extract lacks the hard longevity-outcome trials of a Tier A compound. It is best positioned as a vascular-antioxidant adjunct rather than a stack foundation.

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Start:** 150 mg standardized 95% OPC/day with food
- **Target:** 150–300 mg/day
- **Form:** Verify ≥95% OPC standardization — generic "grape seed" without an OPC percentage is unreliable
- **Timing:** With food; splitting AM/PM is optional
- **Duration:** 12 weeks before rechecking blood pressure and inflammatory markers

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Well tolerated:** Human trials report a strong safety record at 150–300 mg/day.
- **Antiplatelet activity:** OPCs mildly reduce platelet aggregation — relevant on anticoagulants or before surgery.
- **Standardization risk:** Under-standardized products deliver far less active OPC than the label implies.

:::redflag title="Do not self-start without clearance"
- **On anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs** — additive bleeding risk
- **Before scheduled surgery** — pause per your surgeon's guidance
- **Pregnancy or breastfeeding** — insufficient supplemental-dose safety data
- **On antihypertensives** — additive blood-pressure lowering; monitor and coordinate
:::
