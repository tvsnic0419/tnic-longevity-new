> **TL;DR** — Magnesium is a cofactor for over 300 enzymatic reactions, including the ones that activate vitamin D and generate ATP. A large fraction of adults run below optimal intake. Glycinate is the best-tolerated general form (and glycine adds its own sleep benefit); threonate is the specific form with human data for crossing the blood-brain barrier. Tier B; 200–400 mg elemental magnesium/day.

## 1. What magnesium does

Magnesium is a required cofactor for over 300 enzymes, including ATP-producing reactions, DNA/RNA synthesis and repair, and — directly relevant to another module in this library — the enzymes that convert vitamin D to its active hormone form. Despite being essential and abundant in whole foods (leafy greens, nuts, whole grains), a substantial share of adults in modern diets run below recommended intake, and magnesium status tends to decline further with age due to reduced intake and impaired absorption/retention.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** [Mitochondrial dysfunction](/library/mitochondrial-dysfunction) (ATP-generating enzyme cofactor) · [Altered intercellular communication](/library/altered-intercellular-communication) (sleep, neuromuscular signaling) · [Chronic inflammation](/library/chronic-inflammation)

:::evidence tier="B"
Magnesium's cofactor role in ATP production, DNA repair, and vitamin D activation is textbook biochemistry with strong mechanistic backing. Human RCT evidence supports magnesium for sleep quality, blood pressure (modest), and glycemic control in people with low baseline status. Magnesium L-threonate has specific human PK data showing it raises brain magnesium levels more than other forms (Slutsky et al., cited in the compound's cognitive-aging literature) — cognitive-outcome data is earlier-stage. Tier B overall; magnitude of benefit depends heavily on correcting an actual deficit.
:::

:::practical title="Form matters — pick for your goal"
**Glycinate:** best-tolerated general-purpose form (gentle on the gut; glycine adds its own sleep benefit) — the default choice. **Threonate:** the form with human data for raising brain magnesium — pick it if cognition/sleep-onset is the specific target and cost isn't the constraint. **Citrate/oxide:** cheap but more likely to cause GI upset (oxide has notably poor absorption) — oxide is a poor choice for supplementation despite being common on shelves.
:::

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study type | Model | Key outcome | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Magnesium + sleep RCTs | Human, older adults | Improved subjective sleep quality and onset | B |
| Magnesium + blood pressure meta-analyses | Human | Modest SBP/DBP reduction, larger in those with low baseline intake | B |
| Magnesium L-threonate + cognition | Human, early | Signals of benefit; smaller/earlier trials than the sleep/BP literature | C |

**Consensus:** Tier B. Correcting a real intake gap is well supported; the specific-form cognitive claims (threonate) are promising but earlier-stage.

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **General use (glycinate):** 200–400 mg elemental magnesium/day, split AM/PM or taken before bed.
- **Cognitive/sleep-targeted (threonate):** follow label dosing to reach the elemental magnesium studied (typically ~1.5–2 g of the threonate compound, providing ~144 mg elemental Mg).
- **Avoid oxide** for supplementation — poor absorption, more GI upset per elemental mg delivered.
- **Duration:** judge sleep/BP effects at 4–8 weeks.

:::decision title="Which magnesium form, and how much?"
node | Is general deficiency correction / sleep support the goal?
yes | Magnesium glycinate, 200–400 mg elemental/day
no | node | Is cognition or a specific sleep-onset effect the target?
  yes | Magnesium L-threonate at label dose (targets ~144 mg elemental)
  no | Glycinate is still the safest default form
redflag | Kidney disease → magnesium is renally cleared; physician oversight required to avoid accumulation
:::

## 5. Monitoring

Serum magnesium is a poor proxy for total-body status (most magnesium is intracellular/bone), so track function over lab values unless there's a clinical concern.

| Biomarker / measure | Target | Frequency | Action if off-target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sleep quality / onset | Improving | Weekly log, 4–8 wk | Flat → confirm form and dose, check other sleep hygiene factors |
| Blood pressure | Within target range | Monthly | If on antihypertensives, note additive lowering |
| Serum magnesium (if kidney concern) | Normal range | Per physician | Elevated/low → dose adjustment under physician guidance |

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Well tolerated** at typical doses; the main side effect is loose stool (dose-dependent, worse with oxide/citrate).
- **Kidney disease** — magnesium is cleared renally; impaired clearance risks accumulation and toxicity.
- **Drug interactions** — can reduce absorption of some antibiotics (tetracyclines, fluoroquinolones) and bisphosphonates if taken together; space doses by several hours.
- **Additive hypotension** with blood-pressure medication at higher doses.

:::redflag title="Coordinate before starting if any apply"
- **Kidney disease** — physician oversight; accumulation risk
- **On tetracycline/fluoroquinolone antibiotics or bisphosphonates** — space dosing by 2–4 hours
- **On antihypertensives** — monitor for additive blood-pressure lowering
:::
