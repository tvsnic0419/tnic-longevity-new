> **TL;DR** — Selenium is an essential cofactor for glutathione peroxidase and thyroid hormone metabolism, but its cancer-prevention story got a genuine correction from the large SELECT trial (Lippman et al., *JAMA*, 2009, n≈35,000): selenium did **not** reduce prostate cancer risk, and vitamin E in the same trial slightly *increased* it. Selenium remains legitimately important for correcting real deficiency (thyroid, immune function) — it is not a proven cancer-prevention or broad longevity supplement at high doses. Tier B; test before megadosing.

## 1. What selenium does — and the SELECT trial correction

Selenium is incorporated into selenoproteins, including **glutathione peroxidase** (a core antioxidant enzyme) and the enzymes that activate thyroid hormone (T4 → T3). Deficiency is genuinely important where it occurs — it's linked to thyroid dysfunction and impaired immune response — but the compound's popular reputation as a cancer-prevention supplement was substantially built on earlier, smaller observational and trial data that the large, well-designed **SELECT trial** (Selenium and Vitamin E Cancer Prevention Trial) did not confirm.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** [Chronic inflammation](/library/chronic-inflammation) (glutathione peroxidase) · [Altered intercellular communication](/library/altered-intercellular-communication) (thyroid hormone activation)

:::evidence tier="B"
SELECT (Lippman et al., *JAMA*, 2009, n≈35,533 men) — a large, well-designed RCT — found selenium (200 mcg/day) did not reduce prostate cancer incidence, and vitamin E (400 IU/day) was associated with a small but statistically significant *increase* in prostate cancer risk, leading to early trial termination of those arms. This is an important, honest null/negative result for a compound with a strong prior "cancer-prevention" reputation. Selenium's role in correcting genuine deficiency (thyroid function, selenoprotein activity) remains real and Tier B. Do not treat selenium as a cancer-prevention supplement — SELECT is direct evidence against that specific claim.
:::

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study | Design | Key outcomes | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SELECT (Lippman et al., *JAMA* 2009) | Human RCT, n≈35,533 | No prostate-cancer reduction from selenium; vitamin E arm showed increased risk | A (trial quality) / negative result |
| Selenium deficiency-correction literature | Observational + smaller RCTs | Thyroid and immune benefit concentrated in deficient individuals | B |

**Consensus:** Tier B. A real, honest example of a compound whose broad-benefit marketing claim was tested and did not hold up — use it to correct deficiency, not as a cancer-prevention supplement.

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Test first where possible:** dietary selenium intake varies enormously by region (soil selenium content); if deficiency is suspected, testing is reasonable before dosing.
- **General maintenance:** 55–100 mcg/day is adequate for most adults via diet or a modest supplement (2–3 Brazil nuts/day supplies a similar range, with high variability).
- **Avoid megadosing** — the upper safe limit is not far above typical supplemental doses, and selenium toxicity (selenosis) is a real, documented condition.

:::decision title="Should you supplement selenium?"

yes | Supplement to correct (typically 55–100 mcg/day), retest, and taper to maintenance

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Selenosis (toxicity)** is a real, documented condition from chronic excess intake — hair/nail brittleness, GI upset, and in severe cases neurological symptoms.
- **Narrow safety margin** relative to many supplements — do not stack multiple selenium-containing products without totaling the dose.
- **Thyroid medication** — selenium affects thyroid hormone metabolism; coordinate with your prescriber if on thyroid medication.

:::redflag title="Coordinate before starting if any apply"
- **On thyroid medication** — selenium affects hormone-activation enzymes
- **Already consuming high dietary selenium** (e.g., regular Brazil nut intake) — total your intake before adding a supplement
- **Any symptoms of selenosis** (brittle hair/nails, GI upset, nerve symptoms) — stop and seek care
:::
