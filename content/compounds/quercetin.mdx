> **TL;DR** — Quercetin is a flavonoid whose strongest human senolytic evidence comes from the **combination** Dasatinib + Quercetin (D+Q), not quercetin alone. D+Q cleared senescent cells in human adipose tissue (PMID 32854868) and has physician-supervised pilots in IPF and diabetic kidney disease (PMID 30616957). Solo OTC quercetin is a weak standalone senolytic with poor bioavailability — useful as an anti-inflammatory/antioxidant adjunct, not a substitute for dasatinib.

## 1. What quercetin does (and where the evidence actually is)

Quercetin is one of the most-studied dietary flavonoids — abundant in onions, capers, and apples — and it appears in longevity conversations for one specific reason: it is the "Q" in the **D+Q senolytic protocol**. In that pairing, dasatinib (a prescription tyrosine-kinase inhibitor) and quercetin transiently disable the pro-survival networks that keep senescent "zombie" cells alive, letting the cell's own apoptotic machinery clear them. The critical nuance most supplement marketing skips: the human clinical signal is for the **combination**, under physician supervision — not for quercetin taken on its own.

**Primary hallmarks targeted:** [Cellular senescence](/library/cellular-senescence) (senolytic, with dasatinib) · [Chronic inflammation](/library/chronic-inflammation) · [Altered intercellular communication](/library/altered-intercellular-communication) (reduced SASP)

:::evidence tier="B"
The human senolytic evidence is for **D+Q**, not solo quercetin: D+Q reduced senescent-cell burden in human adipose tissue (PMID 32854868) and showed feasibility/physical-function signals in first-in-human pilots for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis and diabetic kidney disease (PMID 30616957). Solo quercetin has not shown a clinically significant standalone senolytic effect in RCTs. Tier B overall — real human combination data plus metabolic/anti-inflammatory evidence, but the senolytic case rests on the physician-supervised pairing.
:::

## 2. Mechanism

| Pathway | Mechanism | Hallmark link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Senolysis (with dasatinib) | Disables pro-survival BCL-family / kinase signaling so primed senescent cells undergo apoptosis | Cellular senescence |
| NF-κB / cytokine modulation | Reduces inflammatory signaling and, indirectly, SASP output | Chronic inflammation |
| Antioxidant / mast-cell stabilization | Scavenges ROS; stabilizes mast cells (the basis of its allergy use) | Communication |
| AMPK activation | Modest metabolic-syndrome and glucose signals in trials | Nutrient sensing |

:::practical title="Where quercetin earns a slot — and where it doesn't"
Quercetin makes sense as a **paired** senolytic (D+Q) only inside a physician-supervised protocol, or as a general **anti-inflammatory/antihistamine adjunct**. It does **not** earn a slot as a solo "anti-aging senolytic" — that claim outruns the evidence. If solo OTC senolytic activity is the goal, [fisetin](/library/compounds/fisetin) has the stronger standalone clinical signal.
:::

## 3. Evidence summary

| Study | Design | Key outcomes | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D+Q adipose-tissue study (PMID 32854868) | Human, senescent-cell burden | Reduced senescent-cell markers in adipose tissue | B |
| D+Q IPF / diabetic-kidney pilots (PMID 30616957) | First-in-human pilots | Feasibility + physical-function / senescence-marker signals | B |
| Solo quercetin senolytic | RCTs | No clinically significant standalone senolytic effect | C |

**Consensus:** Tier B as a **combination** senolytic (D+Q, physician-supervised) and as an anti-inflammatory adjunct. Solo quercetin senolytic claims are Tier C. Do not substitute quercetin for dasatinib when senolysis is the actual goal.

## 4. Dosing protocol

- **Form matters most:** native quercetin (aglycone) is poorly absorbed. Quercetin-3-glucoside (isoquercetin) is better absorbed, and piperine co-administration raises plasma levels.
- **Adjunct dose:** 250–500 mg/day of a bioavailability-optimized form with food.
- **D+Q protocol:** intermittent, physician-directed dosing only — not a daily OTC regimen. Do not self-source dasatinib.
- **Duration:** judge anti-inflammatory/adjunct effects at 8–12 weeks.

## 5. Monitoring

Quercetin has no consumer "level" to chase — track the inflammatory markers it plausibly moves and, for D+Q, defer entirely to the supervising clinician's monitoring.

| Biomarker | Target | Frequency | Action if off-target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| hs-CRP | <1.0 mg/L | Baseline, 12 wk | Persistently high → audit sleep, exercise, and stack, not quercetin dose alone |
| Subjective inflammation/allergy load | Improving | Weekly log | Flat at 12 wk → quercetin is likely not your limiting factor |

## 6. Safety, red flags, and contraindications

- **Adjunct doses are well tolerated**, but high chronic doses have less long-term human safety data.
- **CYP3A4 inhibition** — quercetin can raise levels of drugs metabolized by CYP3A4; review with a prescriber.
- **Antiplatelet effect** — additive bleeding risk with anticoagulant/antiplatelet therapy.
- **Kidney disease** — high-dose flavonoid loading warrants physician oversight.

:::redflag title="Do not self-run a senolytic protocol"
- **Dasatinib is prescription-only** — never self-source it; D+Q must be clinician-directed.
- **On anticoagulants/antiplatelets** — additive bleeding risk from quercetin.
- **On CYP3A4-metabolized medication** — interaction risk; coordinate with your prescriber.
- **Pregnancy or breastfeeding** — insufficient supplemental-dose safety data.
:::
